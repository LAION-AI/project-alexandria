# Evaluation

The question the artifact has to answer: **does the KU chain carry the film's factual content
well enough that a small model can answer detailed questions it could not otherwise answer?**

Protocol follows the published Alexandria MCQ design (`experiments/mcq.py`, `reproduce.py`),
with three additions the screenplay case forces.

## Instrument

**100 questions: 25 scenes × 4.** Scenes sampled stratified by act position and by length
(so the median 45-word scene is represented, not only the set-pieces), with the sample drawn
once from a fixed seed and then **frozen**. Re-picking it later would make new numbers
incomparable with the first run — the storytree project's rule about its fifteen-scene sample,
which is the reason its arms can be compared at all.

Each question: four options, exactly one correct, three distractors that are plausible at the
level of the film and separable **only** by having read that scene. A distractor naming an
event that happens in a different scene is good; one that is impossible on general knowledge
is useless.

Questions are generated by the 27B **from the source scene text**, never from the KUs.
Generating them from KUs would grade the KUs against a list the KU apparatus produced, which
measures compliance rather than correctness, and is error #1 of the eight.

## The confound that matters most

*The Matrix* is one of the most widely described films in existence. A 2026 instruction-tuned
model has read a great deal about it. **A question answerable from memory measures nothing
about the KUs.**

So the no-context arm is not a formality here — it is the instrument's calibration, and it
runs first:

- Every question is answered by the student with **no context**, n=5.
- Questions answered correctly above chance without context are marked `leaky` and reported as
  a separate stratum.
- Headline numbers are reported on the **non-leaky** stratum, with the full set also given.
- If the leaky fraction is large, the instrument is bad and the honest response is to
  regenerate harder questions, not to report the full-set number.

Chance is 25%. A no-context score near 25% means the questions are doing their job.

## Arms

| Arm | Context given to the student |
|---|---|
| `none` | nothing — parametric knowledge only |
| `full_text` | the complete screenplay, 39,637 tokens |
| `ku_chain` | the complete KU chain, no source text |
| `ku_scene` | only the KUs for the questioned scene ±2 *(diagnostic, separates retrieval from representation)* |

Student: `google/gemma-4-E4B-it`, 131k context, served locally. Both `full_text` and
`ku_chain` fit with room to spare — verified before the run, since an arm that silently
truncates would produce a confident wrong comparison.

`full_text` is the upper bound, `none` the floor. The claim under test is that `ku_chain`
lands near `full_text` while the artifact contains no source expression.

## Sampling and position bias

Two things the published protocol does not do, added because without them the comparison is
not trustworthy at this scale:

**n=5 samples per question per arm**, temperature 0.7, rather than one greedy sample.
Single-sample cells leave within-condition variance unmeasured, and it may exceed the
differences being reported — the explicit standing caveat on the storytree scene results.
Reported as mean with a bootstrap 95% CI over questions.

**Option order shuffled per sample**, with the correct answer's position recorded. Small
instruction-tuned models have a substantial position prior; unshuffled, an arm can win on
letter bias alone. Per-position accuracy is reported so the bias is visible rather than
absorbed into the headline.

## Answer extraction

`extract_historical_choice()` from `experiments/mcq.py`, which accepts the legacy `;A;` form
and a bare letter. Unparseable responses are counted as a **separate category, never as
wrong** — scoring a parse failure as an incorrect answer silently penalizes whichever arm
produces longer outputs.

## Reported

Per arm: mean accuracy ± CI on non-leaky and full strata, parse-failure rate, per-position
accuracy, mean context tokens, wall-clock.

Plus the artifact-level numbers from [03-checks.md](03-checks.md) — in particular the C2
overlap distribution, because "the KUs scored well" and "the KUs contain no source expression"
are two claims and this work needs both.

## What this design cannot show

- **One film, one student, one extraction model.** Nothing here generalizes to other
  screenplays, and it should not be written up as if it does.
- **The question generator and the KU extractor are the same model.** They see different
  inputs and the questions come from source text, so this is not circular — but a shared
  blind spot in Qwen3.8 would be invisible to this design. A second generator model would
  settle it and is the obvious next run.
- **MCQ measures recognition, not reconstruction.** That the temporal order is *recoverable*
  is checked mechanically by C4, not by this instrument.
