Project Alexandria · Screenplay extension

Can screenplay Knowledge Units be shared?

An honest reading of where structured narrative extraction sits under copyright — including the part of the original Alexandria argument that does not carry over from scientific papers to dramatic works.

Prepared for LAION research & counsel Subject screenplay/ KU chain Date 19 August 2026
Not legal advice. This is a technical author's structured summary of the considerations, written to give counsel a starting point and to stop the engineering work from making claims it cannot support. It is not written by a lawyer, it does not cover every jurisdiction, and nothing here should be relied on for a release decision.
§1

The question actually being asked

The pipeline converts a screenplay into 225 Knowledge Units: ordered beats, entity states, per-scene context. No dialogue survives as dialogue — the longest verbatim run anywhere in the artifact is seven words, measured across 14,989 fields by an independently written audit.

That measurement answers one question well and a different question not at all. It shows the artifact does not redistribute the screenplay's language. It says nothing about whether the artifact redistributes the screenplay's structure — and for a dramatic work, structure is protected too.

The overlap gate is evidence about expression, not a copyright clearance. Reporting it as the latter would be the same category error the project has spent its checks trying to avoid.

§2

Where the Alexandria argument stops working

The original paper rests on a clean and correct principle: facts are not protectable expression. That photosynthesis releases oxygen is not owned by the textbook that states it, so a Knowledge Unit capturing it redistributes nothing protectable, no matter how complete.

Fiction breaks the principle at its root. The events of a screenplay are not discovered facts about the world — they are authored inventions. Courts call them “fictional facts” and treat them as expression, precisely because there was nothing there to discover.

Scientific paper

The fact existed before the paper. Extracting it takes something the author never owned. Completeness is not an aggravating factor.

Screenplay

The “fact” was invented by the author. Extracting it takes the authored thing itself. Completeness makes the taking larger, not safer.

This is the single most important line in this document, and it inverts the intuition the engineering work naturally builds: the more faithfully and completely the units capture the narrative, the more of the protected work they carry. Fidelity is a legal liability here in a way it never was for the paper corpus.

§3

The authorities that cut against

US case law is unusually direct on this, and the closest case is close indeed.

Castle Rock Entertainment v. Carol Publishing Group — 150 F.3d 132 (2d Cir. 1998)

A trivia book of questions about Seinfeld — pure facts about the show, no script reproduced — was held infringing, and fair use was rejected. The court held the “fictional facts” were original expression owned by the rightsholder. This is the nearest analogue to an MCQ instrument plus a fact extraction, and it went against the defendant.

Twin Peaks Productions v. Publications International — 996 F.2d 1366 (2d Cir. 1993)

Detailed plot summaries of episodes exceeded fair use. Abridgement was found to be a derivative work where it recounted the plot in sufficient detail — which is a fair description of an ordered scene-by-scene beat chain.

Nichols v. Universal Pictures — 45 F.2d 119 (2d Cir. 1930)

Learned Hand's abstractions test: protection extends beyond literal text to the pattern of incident and character, up to the level where it becomes unprotected abstraction. A beat chain deliberately sits below that line — it is specific by design.

Warner Bros. v. RDR Books — 575 F. Supp. 2d 513 (S.D.N.Y. 2008)

The Harry Potter Lexicon. Useful in both directions: a reference work reorganising a fictional universe was held capable of being transformative in purpose, but infringed because the amount taken exceeded what that purpose required. The lesson is that purpose alone does not save volume.

§4

The grounds that support sharing

These are real and, for a non-profit research organisation, substantial — but note carefully what each one covers.

Text and data mining exemptions

EU DSM Directive Article 3 permits reproductions and extractions by research organisations for scientific TDM, cannot be overridden by contract, and permits retention for verification. Article 4 offers a broader exemption subject to rightsholder opt-out. Germany implements at UrhG §60d; Japan's Article 30-4 is broader still.

The limit that matters: Article 3 authorises making the copies needed to do the analysis. It is not, on its face, a publication right for outputs that themselves contain protected expression. It protects the extraction; it does not automatically protect the redistribution.

Non-expressive / non-consumptive use

Authors Guild v. Google (804 F.3d 202, 2d Cir. 2015) and Authors Guild v. HathiTrust (755 F.3d 87, 2d Cir. 2014) sustained mass digitisation for search and analysis. The strength of those holdings tracks how little of the expression reached the public: Google Books won substantially because snippet view was deliberately crippled as a substitute for reading the book.

That is the operative test to apply here, and it is a good one: does the artifact substitute for experiencing the work? A frequency table does not. A complete ordered account of every scene begins to.

Transformation of purpose

Google v. Oracle (141 S. Ct. 1183, 2021) affirmed that reuse for a genuinely different function weighs heavily. Training data for narrative understanding is a different purpose from entertainment — but Castle Rock is the reminder that a different purpose did not save a work that repackaged the fictional content itself.

§5

A risk gradient, by what you release

Risk is not binary across the artifact — it varies sharply with granularity and with whether narrative order is preserved. Ordering is the hinge: it is what turns a set of facts into a recounting.

Release formBandReasoning
Model weights trained on the units; aggregate statistics Lowest No expression recoverable; squarely non-expressive use.
The pipeline, schema, checks and MCQ instrument — no corpus Lowest Nothing derived from the protected work is distributed at all.
Entity inventory, character graph, unordered attributes Low Closest to unprotected idea; no narrative recounted.
Per-scene facts with scene order removed or coarsened Medium Substantial detail, but does not recount the work as a sequence.
Ordered scene-by-scene beat chain — the current artifact Medium-high Functionally a very detailed scene-by-scene synopsis. This is the Twin Peaks zone, and completeness is what puts it there. ← we are here
Anything permitting reconstruction of dialogue or prose Highest Direct substitution; no exemption reaches it.

The uncomfortable observation is that the project's quality goal and its legal exposure point the same way. Every improvement that makes the units better at preserving the narrative moves the artifact down this table.

§7

Options, ranked by how much they actually solve

  1. Ship the pipeline, not the corpus solves it

    Release the code, schema, checks and instrument; let each research group derive units locally from a copy they lawfully hold. Nothing derived from a protected work is distributed, the TDM exemption covers each group's own extraction, and reproducibility survives intact via source_url and source_sha256. This is the strongest available answer and costs the project almost nothing.

  2. Move to public-domain or licensed source texts solves it

    The method is not specific to The Matrix. Demonstrating it on a public-domain play or an openly licensed screenplay removes the question entirely and makes the published corpus reusable without caveat.

  3. Seek permission for the demonstration corpus solves it

    A single rightsholder clearance for one screenplay, used as a published worked example, converts the whole question into a licensing fact.

  4. Gate access to research organisations reduces it

    Aligns distribution with the population Article 3 contemplates and limits substitution. It narrows exposure; it does not resolve whether the artifact is a derivative work.

  5. Publish a reduced form: drop global ordering reduces it

    Ordering is what makes the chain a recounting. Releasing per-scene units without the cross-scene sequence moves up the gradient — but it also removes the temporal spine that is the extension's main contribution, so this is a real cost, not a free mitigation.

The first three are genuine solutions and two of them are nearly free. Given that, there is little reason to rest the project on the contested ground at all.

§8

Questions for counsel

  1. Does an ordered, complete, scene-by-scene beat chain constitute a derivative work under Twin Peaks, notwithstanding that no protected language survives?
  2. Does Article 3 DSM support publishing TDM outputs of this granularity, or only performing the analysis and retaining copies for verification?
  3. How much does Castle Rock constrain publishing an MCQ instrument built from a copyrighted fictional work, even where the questions quote nothing?
  4. Does the three-scene worked-examples exhibit qualify as scholarly quotation in the jurisdictions LAION publishes from (including InfoSoc Art. 5(3)(d) quotation)?
  5. Does releasing pipeline-only, with the corpus derived locally by each recipient, fully discharge the concern — and is that conclusion stable across the EU, US and UK?
§9

Recommendation

Publish the method, withhold the corpus, and move the demonstration to a source that carries no question.

The scientific contribution here is the pipeline, the check design, and the finding that retrieval dominates representation — none of which requires distributing units derived from a copyrighted screenplay. The Matrix chain can stay on the machine that made it and be cited by digest, with the numbers fully reproducible by anyone holding their own lawful copy.

What should not happen is publishing the chain while citing the seven-word overlap figure as the justification. That number is true, it was hard won, and it answers a question nobody is going to ask.
