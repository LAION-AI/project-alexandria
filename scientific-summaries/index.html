02 / How we evaluate

Can another model answer
questions from the representation?

We measure answerability: how well a fixed question-answering model can recover facts using a summary, Knowledge Units or the available original text.

  1. 1

    Freeze the questions

    The benchmark contains 97 papers and 970 four-choice questions—ten per paper. Questions were created from source text, before the tested representations were generated.

  2. 2

    Generate without the answers

    Summary and KU generators receive source text. They never see the test questions, gold answers, rationales or QA evidence annotations.

  3. 3

    Ask the same student

    The fixed student receives each question with one selected context. Accuracy is the number of correct answers divided by all 970 questions.

Q

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct

This is the evaluation model. Gemma and Qwen 27B generate the representations; Qwen2.5 answers the questions.

Protocol, uncertainty and what the score does not prove

The 97-paper set has 50 arXiv and 47 Bethgelab records. Its synthetic questions were authored by gpt-6-luna from source text. It is a length-filtered convenience sample; available text was not independently verified against complete publisher originals.

The student uses temperature 0.5, top-p 0.95, a 100-token output cap and frequency/presence penalties of 1.05. The historical answer prompt, ASCII sanitizer and parser are retained, with at most five formatting attempts. An answer that remains invalid is wrong. Failed generation keeps all ten questions for that paper in the denominator.

Confidence intervals resample whole papers, keeping each paper’s ten questions together. The answerer is stochastic, so unchanged texts can receive slightly different scores in another run. Scores measure answerability under this student and synthetic benchmark; they are not a human certification of factual correctness. Repeated benchmark use also limits independence.

08 / Better paraphrasing

Preserve the facts.
Reduce copied wording.

Fluent summaries can still reproduce passages from their source. We tested backtranslation and direct Gemma 4B editing as a repair stage after generation, measuring both the remaining copied wording and the information available to the QA student.

OUR PARAPHRASING TARGET

At most five consecutive source words in narrative prose. A match of six or more words fails this strict target. The audit compares each summary with its own full available source; it reports narrative prose, evidence quotations and bibliographic metadata separately.

This is an engineering rule, not a legal copyright threshold. An overlap can be a conventional scientific phrase, so the detector identifies wording to inspect rather than deciding whether a passage is improper.

Two ways to rewrite a flagged passage

TRANSLATE AND RETURN

TranslateGemma 4B
backtranslation

We take a flagged English passage with nearby sentence context, translate it into German, and translate the German back into English. The round trip can change wording and sentence structure while retaining the original information.

The reference uses greedy decoding. The sampled experiment uses temperature 0.35 and top-p 0.95, with either one attempt or up to two. A second attempt is made only after the first is rejected; it starts again from the original English rather than translating an already modified passage.

A sampled candidate is accepted only if both translations finish, the protected signatures match, its longest source match is at most five words, and the entailment check passes in both directions. Otherwise the original passage stays.

TranslateGemma model card ↗
EDIT IN ENGLISH

Gemma 4 E4B IT
direct repair

A small instruction model rewrites the selected English passage directly. It receives a short context window around the copying, with instructions to preserve the scientific meaning, rather than generating a new whole-paper summary.

We tested one pass and an iterative policy with up to 20 attempts, thinking disabled, and a temperature schedule of 0.35, 0.65 and 0.95. Candidates are checked against the immutable pre-repair passage.

This policy can retain safe partial progress: copied-word coverage must decrease without increasing the longest match, protected signatures must remain unchanged, and both entailment checks must pass. Unresolved matches remain visible in the audit.

Gemma repair model card ↗

Three different roles: Gemma 4 12B with the rank-128 summary adapter generates the initial text. TranslateGemma 4B or Gemma 4 E4B IT repairs selected passages. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct answers the evaluation questions. The repair models do not receive the questions or gold answers.

What happens between generation and evaluation?

  1. 01

    Scan the source

    Find contiguous matches of at least six normalized whitespace words. Keep separate summary statements separate, and audit against untruncated available source text.

  2. 02

    Keep the context

    Select the affected sentence window with preceding context. Edit narrative prose; preserve literal evidence quotations and bibliography, and audit them separately.

  3. 03

    Check each rewrite

    Compare numeric, unit and formula signatures with the original window. A separate entailment model must score at least 0.90 in both directions.

  4. 04

    Check the assembled text

    Validate the structure, protected fields, document signatures and longest matches after inserting edits. Revert unsafe outputs and keep them in the results.

  5. 05

    Measure both outcomes

    Re-audit the saved summary and ask the fixed QA student the same frozen questions. QA scores never choose an edit, a retry budget or which papers to retain.

What “coverage” means

For each summary, count the narrative words covered by any source match of six or more words, counting each word once. Divide by its narrative word count, then average across the 97 summaries. Lower is better. The reduction underneath is relative to the original summary’s 15.23% coverage.

What “strict pass” means

A whole summary passes only when no narrative passage contains a source match longer than five words. 7.71% copied-word coverage does not mean only 7.71% of papers fail. Every tested summary still has a long narrative match: strict passes are 0/97 in every row.

WHAT WE LEARNED
  • Iterative direct Gemma repair gives the largest reduction in this comparison: copied-word coverage falls by 49.3%, from 15.23% to 7.71%. Its QA score is 93.81%, versus 94.12% for the original summaries. The paired interval includes zero, so this run does not establish either a quality loss or equivalence.
  • A second sampled backtranslation attempt reduces coverage further but also lowers QA: 9.60% → 9.09% coverage, and 93.40% → 93.09% QA. The two-attempt result is 1.03 percentage points below the original, with a paired interval of −1.86 to −0.21. More attempts are not an automatic improvement.
  • The strict target remains unsolved. Rejecting a rewrite preserves the original wording. Protected quotations also remain unchanged: 34/97 summaries have an evidence match of at least seven words, separately from the narrative metrics above. These methods reduce copying; they do not certify complete paraphrasing.
Exact settings, numeric checks and the wider 1,000-version repair test

The direct editor runs in BF16 with thinking disabled, up to 128 active sequences, length-sorted prompts, prefix caching and chunked prefill. Its context limit is 4,096 tokens and its output cap is 768 tokens. The sampled translator runs in BF16 with batches up to 512, a 2,048-token context limit and a 768-token output cap. A declared retry budget is a ceiling, not a promise that every passage is rewritten that many times.

The entailment filter is DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli. It checks whether the original supports the candidate and whether the candidate supports the original, each at a 0.90 threshold. Numeric, unit and formula signatures provide an additional conservative filter. Retained repairable outputs have zero detected critical-signature drift, but these checks cannot verify every mathematical expression or prove scientific equivalence.

The wider direct-Gemma stress test processed 1,000 summary versions from 962 unique papers: 865 Qwen teacher drafts, 97 FP8 evaluation summaries and 38 BF16 versions of evaluation papers. One pre-existing unparseable input was retained, leaving 999 auditable versions. These groups are not pooled into the 97-paper QA table.

Across both direct-Gemma conditions, whole-document guards reverted 33 output variants covering 19 distinct summary-version identifiers. Reverted texts remain in the results. No papers are dropped to improve the score, and QA never selects a repair. Evidence quotations and metadata retain their original wording; their overlap is separately reported rather than silently exempted from the audit.

Model revisions, seeds, acceptance policies, paired intervals and exact coverage values are available in the machine-readable benchmark data. Five words is a project convention; it is not a guarantee of copyright clearance, scientific correctness or independent human approval.

A SEPARATE APPROACH · PROCEDURAL REPAIR V3

Local rewording without a generative editor

This variant uses context-checked substitutions, typed phrase rules and descriptor reordering. It protects numbers, formulas, logical qualifiers, names and quotations, and reverts unsafe edits. It reduces mean narrative copied-word coverage by 39.3%, with no generative model call in the repair stage.

Twenty-two million summaries.

We plan to summarize as many additional papers as feasible, expand scientific coverage and improve paraphrasing. Open adapters, inspectable examples and transparent evaluation are part of that work.
