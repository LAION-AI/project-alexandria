Recorded context summary

A randomized controlled study evaluated the impact of a six-month fasting-mimicking diet on metabolic markers and albuminuria in patients with type 2 diabetes and diabetic nephropathy. The intervention group showed significant improvements in microalbuminuria and insulin resistance compared to a Mediterranean diet control group. These benefits were partially sustained at follow-up, while markers for DNA damage and dicarbonyl detoxification remained unchanged.

While FMD induced greater weight loss, body composition changes were not significantly different between groups, and most metabolic improvements were not sustained at follow-up. Next KU: The study concludes that while FMD reduced microalbuminuria and improved metabolic markers, it did not activate specific defense pathways for nephropathy reversal, suggesting longer intervention periods may be required. Limitations include the caloric restriction in the FMD group compared to the isocaloric control, though statistical adjustments were made for weight loss.
