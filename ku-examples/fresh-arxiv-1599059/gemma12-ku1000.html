Recorded context summary

The paper proposes an adiabatic quantum search algorithm using an ensemble of three-level atoms in a single-mode cavity driven by two lasers. The method utilizes an inverse fractional stimulated Raman adiabatic passage (if-STIRAP) to transition from an entangled initial state to a marked state. This process enables Grover's speedup, where the required time scales proportionally to the square root of the database size.

Recorded context summary

The system Hamiltonian for N atoms in a cavity is block-diagonalized by the number of photons, with the single-photon block supporting the Grover search. A unitary transformation maps the system to a basis of uniform superpositions of unmarked and marked states, allowing for the derivation of an effective Hamiltonian. The resonant approximation and adiabatic elimination are applied to simplify the dynamics, resulting in an effective Hamiltonian valid when the pulse amplitude is small relative to the coupling strength.

Recorded context summary

The effective Hamiltonian for the cavity-laser-atom system is analyzed in a basis that facilitates adiabatic population transfer from a uniform superposition to a marked state. The process requires simultaneous pulse activation and specific pulse shaping to maintain a constant ratio between the adiabatic parameter and the energy gap. Analytical and numerical results confirm that the process duration scales as the square root of the database size while maintaining high transfer efficiency.
