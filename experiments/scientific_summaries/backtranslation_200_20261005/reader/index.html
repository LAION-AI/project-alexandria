Multi-spectral Observations of Lunar Occultations: I. Resolving The Dust Shell Around AFGL 5440

Paul M. Harvey, Andrew Oldag

Observational paper with modeling2,340 narrative words~11 min read
Read the paper ↗Download saved outputs ↓
Executive summary

This paper presents high-speed multi-spectral lunar occultation observations of AFGL 5440, a dust-enshrouded carbon-rich AGB star at a distance of 2.25 kpc with luminosity 1.4×10⁴ L⊙. Observations were conducted over 1–4 µm using pMIRAS, a prototype high-speed spectrophotometer on the McDonald Observatory 2.7-m telescope, during an immersion event on 28 August 2001 with lunar phase 0.72 and position angle 54°. The time resolution of 8 msec enables an effective angular resolution of a couple milli-arcsec, allowing simultaneous observation of the Fresnel fringe pattern of the obscured central star and the circumstellar dust emission from the warmest region of the shell. The authors demonstrate that a single-source-size model cannot fit the 1–4 µm data across the entire band, revealing a combination of stellar emission and circumstellar shell emission. Using the DUSTY radiative transfer code, more than 200 models were tested, varying dust temperature, density gradient, optical depth, and composition. The most suitable model yielded a maximum dust temperature Tmax = 950 K ± 50 K, an optical depth at 5 µm of 0.5 ± 0.1, and a radial density gradient that corresponds to ρ ∝ r⁻², which is consistent with a radiation-driven hydrodynamic outflow. The physical inner radius of the dust cloud was limited to 3.7 × 10¹⁴ cm (11 mas angular diameter), which is approximately twice the stellar diameter of 3 mas. A dust loss rate of 6.5 × 10⁻⁸ M⊙ per year was determined, which is consistent with the estimate of 5.1 × 10⁻⁸ M⊙ per year, based on IRAS data, within the bounds of uncertainty. However, the models could not fit the IRAS 60 µm flux without assuming substantial graphite or a past higher mass-loss rate, implying that the mass-loss rate may have decreased over 500–2000 years. Two comparison stars, IRC+00233 and HD 155292, were observed to calibrate the analysis, yielding best-fit sizes of 4.5–5.0 mas and <3 mas, respectively, consistent with blackbody expectations.

Design of Real-time Semantic Segmentation Decoder for Automated Driving

Arindam Das, Saranya Kandan, Senthil Yogamani, Pavel Křížek

Technical Research / Architecture Design2,277 narrative words~10 min read
Read the paper ↗Download saved outputs ↓
Executive summary

This paper addresses the computational bottleneck in decoders for semantic segmentation in embedded systems for autonomous driving, where it is assumed that a task-independent and efficient encoder provides shared features for multi-task-oriented systems. The authors propose a novel, non-bottleneck-based layer based on residual learning with nested skip connections, designed to handle high gradient flows and extract semantic information from a generic, non-specialized encoder. Using a VGG10 style classifier (10 layers) as a task-independent encoder with 256 feature maps at the final layer, the authors design and evaluate a family of decoder architectures under a fixed run-time budget. The decoder utilizes deconvolution layers for reconstruction, supplemented by two variants of the non-bottleneck layer: type-1 (combining 3x3, 5x5, and 1x1 kernels) and type-2 (adding dilated convolutions with 3x3 and 5x5 kernels). The encoder produces 256 feature maps, which are downsampled to 4 due to the four-class task (lanes, curb, road, void). Skip connections are pulled from the second and third-to-last encoder convolution layers. The proposed optimal configuration, combining various types of non-bottleneck layers between deconvolution stages, achieves a mean IoU of 0.7441 with a class accuracy of 0.6118 (Lanes), 0.6588 (Curb), and 0.9689 (Road). This represents a 10% absolute improvement in mean IoU over a baseline configuration, with higher gains for difficult classes (12% for lanes, 18% for curb). The decoder improves the IoU (Intersection over Union) values at the class level, without requiring more than 2 milliseconds of processing time on custom hardware. The work contributes a generic decoder design applicable to any semantic segmentation task, demonstrating that architectural choices in the decoder are critical for efficiency-constrained applications.

The study uses an organization-owned dataset of 4,999 images (1280x384 resolution) with urban scenes, divided into 3,016 training, 981 validation, and 1,002 test images. Training involved pre-training the VGG10 encoder on ImageNet, followed by transfer learning with 350K iterations using the Adam optimizer, 0.0005 initial learning rate with polynomial decay, batch size of 4, and weight decay of 0.9. No data augmentation was applied. Results are reported as average class accuracy and IoU across four classes. The paper concludes that decoder design plays a critical role in segmentation efficiency and proposes meta-learning for automated architecture search as future work.

Executive summary

This paper presents a comprehensive physico-chemical and electrochemical characterization of highly concentrated aqueous binary CH₃COOK/CH₃COONa electrolytes, targeting the development of water-in-salt (WISE) electrolytes for sodium-ion batteries. The authors prepared four binary solutions with a fixed CH₃COOK concentration of 20 mol kg⁻¹ and varying CH₃COONa concentrations (1, 2.5, 5, and 7 mol kg⁻¹), alongside a 1 M NaAc control. The series was characterized via Differential Scanning Calorimetry (-120 to 90 °C), rheology (viscosity vs. temperature), electrical impedance spectroscopy (conductivity), and linear sweep voltammetry (electrochemical stability window). Thermal analysis revealed that glass transition temperature (Tg) increases with salt concentration, correlating with the transition from salt-in-water to water-in-salt behavior; notably, the 7N20K composition (7 mol kg⁻¹ NaAc + 20 mol kg⁻¹ KAc) showed a low liquidus temperature (~0 °C), suggesting resistance to salt crystallization. The ionic conductivity exhibited behavior consistent with the Vogel-Tamman-Fulcher laws in all compositions. The most concentrated 7N20K solution had a conductivity of 21.2 mS cm⁻¹ at 25 °C, which is comparable to that of organic systems. Rheological data classified the acetates as structure-making salts with Arrhenius viscosity-temperature dependence. Electrochemical stability window measurements showed suppression of hydrogen evolution at higher concentrations, with 7N20K reaching 3 V in ideal flooded-cell conditions. Impedance spectroscopy revealed a low charge-transfer activation energy of 0.12 eV and a diffusion coefficient of ~10⁻¹³ cm² s⁻¹ at room temperature, confirming the kinetic feasibility of the NASICON/WISE interface for sodium-ion battery applications.

Executive summary

This study provides two cryo-EM structures of endogenously phosphorylated Saccharomyces cerevisiae Ycf1 (a glutathione transporter of the ABC-C subfamily) at 3.4Å and 4.0Å resolution, capturing previously unobserved ordered states of the intrinsically disordered regulatory R-domain (residues 901-935) in two inward-facing conformations (IFwide and IFnarrow). The R-domain, phosphorylated at S908, T911, and S914, is shown to be helical at its C-terminus (residues 920-929) and forms extensive electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions with NBD1 (spanning three buried hydrophobic sites) and the lasso domain. Biochemical data, including ATPase assays, in-gel phosphoprotein staining, limited proteolysis, and LC-MS/MS, confirm that R-domain phosphorylation reorganizes the Ycf1 architecture and is required for maximal ATPase activity. The dephosphorylated state shows a more complete protease digestion pattern and drastically reduced ATPase activity, consistent with cellular phenotypes where S908 and T911 mutations impair cadmium detoxification. The R-domain encircles NBD1 in both structures, with the IFwide-to-IFnarrow transition involving a rigid body movement of the R-domain/NBD1 complex relative to NBD2 and a reconfiguration of the ATP-binding sites, including partial occlusion of the non-functional ATP site in NBD1. The R-domain phosphorylation sites align with PKA-like (RRAS) and CKII kinase recognition motifs. A conserved, inhibitory phosphosite at position S251 (Lasso domain) is identified nearby and is poised to disrupt the interaction of the R domain through electrostatic repulsion. These findings challenge existing models of the transition from disordered to ordered R domain and provide mechanistic insights for the ABCC transporter family, with clinical relevance for CFTR and the binding of Lumacaftor.

Executive summary

This paper, authored by A.M. Mathai and H.J. Haubold, provides a comprehensive theoretical investigation into generalized entropy measures and their applications. The authors first establish that the product probability property (PPP), or statistical independence, is the fundamental basis upon which Shannon entropy is constructed. They demonstrate that the logarithmic form of Shannon's entropy is based on a specific recursive postulate: If the m-th event in a partition is divided into independent events, the resulting entropy is a weighted sum, which structurally requires a logarithmic representation. The paper formalizes five generalized entropies of order α—Rényi (1961), Havrda-Charvát (1967), Tsallis (1988), Mathai (2006), and Mathai's additive entropy (2006)—all of which converge to Shannon entropy as α → 1.

Key theoretical results include the non-additivity relations for joint distributions, where the entropy of a product distribution equals the sum of marginal entropies plus an α-dependent interaction term. The authors also derive a trivariate extension of these non-additivity relations. A central methodology is the Maximum Entropy Principle applied via calculus of variation to each continuous entropy functional. Under different moment constraints, the authors show that Mathai's entropy recovers the entire hierarchy of distributions: generalized type-1 beta, type-2 beta, Tsallis statistics, stretched exponentials, and even the Maxwell-Boltzmann, Gaussian, Weibull, and Wigner functions as special cases. Furthermore, the authors prove that Mathai's entropy satisfies a specific second-order ordinary differential equation that generalizes the power law of Tsallis statistics. The work concludes by connecting Mathai's entropy to Kerridge's measure of inaccuracy, framing it as a type of directed divergence measure.
