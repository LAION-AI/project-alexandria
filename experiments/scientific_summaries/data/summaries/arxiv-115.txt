Executive Summary
This paper presents a theoretical analysis of the Coulomb drag transresistivity in double-layer two-dimensional electron gases (2DEGs) within the quantum Hall regime near a filling factor of ν = 1/2. The study investigates the temperature and magnetic field dependencies of transresistivity, focusing on the impact of Fermi-liquid interactions between quasiparticles. The central finding is that Fermi-liquid interactions can significantly influence the transresistivity, explaining its observed independence of interlayer spacing for experimentally relevant values. The author derives an expression for the transresistivity, incorporating Fermi-liquid effects, and compares the results with experimental data. The model predicts a nearly quadratic enhancement of transresistivity with deviations from ν = 1/2, consistent with experiments. The study highlights the role of Fermi-liquid interactions in reducing the compressibility of the composite fermion (CF) liquid, thereby increasing the screening length and explaining the insensitivity of transresistivity to interlayer spacing. A key limitation is the reliance on approximations and the scope conditions of the model, particularly at low temperatures. The work is significant because it offers a theoretical framework to understand the behavior of Coulomb drag in double-layer systems, providing insights into the role of many-body interactions in quantum Hall physics and offering a possible explanation for experimental observations.

Research Context
Prior research on Coulomb drag in double-layer 2DEGs has shown a temperature dependence of transresistivity proportional to T^(4/3) at ν = 1/2, attributed to ballistic contributions. Experiments revealed a quadratic enhancement of transresistivity with deviations from ν = 1/2, with curvature dependent on temperature but insensitive to the sign of the deviation or interlayer distance. Existing theoretical models, such as those based on composite fermion (CF) approaches, lacked a complete explanation for the observed behavior, particularly the insensitivity to interlayer spacing. The current work addresses this gap by incorporating Fermi-liquid interactions into the theoretical framework, which were not fully considered in previous studies. This allows for a more comprehensive understanding of the experimental results.

Key Results
The study derives an expression for the transresistivity ρD, which is separated into two terms: ρD0, the transresistivity at ν = 1/2, and δρD, a correction arising from a non-zero effective magnetic field. The expression for ρD0 coincides with a known result: ρD0 = (h/e^2) * (Γ(7/3)ζ(4/3)/3√3) * (T/T0)^(4/3). The leading term of the correction δρD at low temperatures (T/T0) ≪ 1 is given by δρD = (2/3)ρD0 * (1/(kF R)) * (1 + (3/8) * (1/(kF R))) + a2 * (h/e^2) * (2T/T0)^(2/3) * (∆ν)^2, where kF is the Fermi wave vector, R is the CF cyclotron radius, and ∆ν = ν - 1/2. The derived expression predicts a nearly quadratic enhancement of transresistivity with ∆ν, consistent with experimental observations. The analysis shows that the magnetic field dependence of the transresistivity near ν = 1/2 matches that observed in experiments (Figure 1). The ratio ρD/ρD0 can be presented in the form ρD/ρD0 = 4β(∆ν)^2 + 1, where β is a coefficient proportional to the curvature of the plot of Eq. (22). The temperature dependence of β^(-1) also agrees with experimental results (Figure 2). The model provides a possible explanation for the insensitivity of the drag to the interlayer spacing. The Fermi liquid interaction provides a significant reduction of the compressibility of the CF liquid, and a consequent enhancement in the screening length in single layers. The results show a reasonably good agreement between the theoretical predictions and the experimental results, using A0 = 15 and A1 = 3 (m* = 4mb).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that Fermi-liquid interactions play a crucial role in the behavior of Coulomb drag in double-layer 2DEGs near ν = 1/2. The model's ability to reproduce the experimental results, particularly the quadratic dependence on ∆ν and the insensitivity to interlayer spacing, supports the hypothesis that these interactions are a key factor. The study implies that the Fermi-liquid interactions significantly reduce the compressibility of the CF liquid, enhancing the screening length within the layers. This enhanced screening, in turn, explains why the transresistivity is less sensitive to the distance between the layers. The work provides a theoretical basis for understanding the experimental observations and offers insights into the role of many-body interactions in quantum Hall systems. The results suggest that the experimental data can be used to estimate the strength of Fermi-liquid interactions in the CF system near ν = 1/2, which is important for further studies.
