Executive Summary
This study presents a metal-free photocatalytic oxidative coupling (POC) method for synthesizing azoaromatics from arylamines at charged aqueous interfaces (CAIs) using Eosin Y (EY) as a photocatalyst. The method utilizes micelles formed by cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) to host both the photocatalyst and the arylamine substrates, facilitating a non-diffusive photoinduced electron transfer (PET) process. The research demonstrates that this approach achieves excellent yields (up to 100%) and selectivity (100%) under ambient air conditions, with a reaction time of 4 hours. The catalyst loading is low, at 0.6 mole% of EY. The study's novelty lies in its use of CAIs to enhance the reaction kinetics and substrate scope, outperforming existing methods. The key finding is the significant boosting of catalytic efficacy due to the non-diffusive PET and modulation of redox potentials at the CAI. The primary limitation is the focus on specific substrates and micelle types. This work opens opportunities for sustainable synthesis of azoaromatics.

Research Context
The synthesis of azoaromatics is important in organic chemistry, but traditional methods using stoichiometric amounts of nitrile salts produce unstable diazonium intermediates and waste. Metal nanoparticle-catalyzed synthesis suffers from slow kinetics, poor selectivity, and high temperatures. Photocatalytic reductive coupling of nitrobenzenes has poor product selectivity and requires an inert atmosphere. Prior work using Ir-based photocatalysts for POC of arylamines to azoaromatics has limitations including slow reaction kinetics, moderate yields, high catalyst dose, and a limited substrate scope. This research addresses the need for a more efficient and green catalytic route for azo compound synthesis in an aqueous medium under ambient conditions with high selectivity and yields.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research question is whether a metal-free photocatalytic oxidative coupling of arylamines to azoaromatics can be achieved efficiently in an aqueous medium under ambient conditions, using a micellar system to enhance the reaction. The primary hypothesis is that the use of charged aqueous interfaces (CAIs) of micelles, specifically CTAB micelles, will facilitate a non-diffusive photoinduced electron transfer (PET) process between the photocatalyst Eosin Y (EY) and arylamine substrates, leading to high yields and selectivity. Alternative hypotheses include that the reaction may not occur efficiently in water, that the choice of micelle will impact the reaction, or that the reaction kinetics will be slow. The study aims to demonstrate that the micellar environment will enhance the reaction rate and substrate scope compared to previous methods. The study also aims to show that the reaction will be efficient with a low catalyst loading and under ambient conditions, contrasting with previous methods that required higher temperatures or inert atmospheres.

Methodological Details
The study employed a photocatalytic oxidative coupling (POC) methodology to synthesize azoaromatics from arylamines. The reaction was conducted in an aqueous medium using Eosin Y (EY) as a photocatalyst, K3PO4 as a base, and cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) micelles. The primary experimental design involved the optimization of reaction conditions, including the concentration of CTAB, the type of base, and the light source. The study used p-anisidine as a model substrate to optimize the reaction conditions. The reaction was triggered by green light (λ = 525 nm) under ambient air. The conversion of p-anisidine to 1,2-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)diazene was monitored using GC-MS measurements. The photophysical behavior of EY in the presence of CTAB, sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), and triton X-100 (TX-100) was studied using UV-Vis absorption spectroscopy and fluorescence spectroscopy. Fluorescence lifetime measurements were performed to assess the interaction of EY with the micelles. Fluorescence quenching experiments were conducted to validate the photoinduced electron transfer (PET) phenomenon. Fluorescence up-conversion measurements were used to determine the PET time constant. Radical trap experiments were performed using 2,6-di-tert-buty-4-methylphenol (BHT) to validate the formation of amine radicals via the PET mechanism. The products were analyzed using HR-MS and GC-MS measurements. The study did not mention preregistration or multiple-comparison corrections. The study was conducted under ambient conditions. Key Results
The optimized reaction conditions resulted in 100% conversion of p-anisidine to the desired azo product within 4 hours, using 0.6 mol% EY, 2 equiv. K3PO4, and 40 mM CTAB (Table 1). The reaction was also feasible with blue light, but required 6 hours for 100% conversion (Figure S5). Arylamines with electron-rich amine centers showed 100% conversion within 4 hours (Scheme 2a). Aniline also showed 100% conversion. p-chloroaniline and p-bromoaniline showed 98% and 95% conversion, respectively (Scheme 2a). The fluorescence lifetime of EY increased from 1.17 ns in water to 2.74 ns upon micellization of CTAB (Figure 1d and Table S2). A linear Stern-Volmer (SV) plot with a quenching rate constant (kq) of 6.3 × 10^12 M^-1 s^-1 was observed, suggesting a non-diffusive bimolecular quenching process (Figure 2b). Fluorescence up-conversion measurements revealed an ultrafast PET with a time constant of 0.3 ps in the presence of p-anisidine (Figure 2d). The presence of BHT resulted in negligible conversion (~5.0%) to the desired azo product even after 4 hours of irradiation (Scheme 3a).

Interpretation Implications
The results suggest that the CAI of CTAB micelles significantly enhances the POC of arylamines to azoaromatics. The enhanced reaction kinetics and substrate scope are attributed to the non-diffusive PET process facilitated by the micellar environment. The study indicates that the micellar surface modulates the redox potentials, further boosting the reaction efficiency. The findings support the hypothesis that the micellar system provides a favorable microenvironment for the photocatalyst and substrates, leading to high yields and selectivity. The ultrafast PET observed in the study suggests a highly efficient electron transfer process. The radical trap experiments validate the amine radical coupling mechanism. The study's findings have implications for the development of sustainable and efficient methods for synthesizing azoaromatics, which are important in various applications.

Three Takeaways
(1) The core novel contribution is the development of a metal-free photocatalytic oxidative coupling method for azoaromatic synthesis at charged aqueous interfaces using EY and CTAB micelles, achieving high yields and selectivity under ambient conditions.

(2) The strongest evidence is the 100% conversion of p-anisidine to the desired azo product within 4 hours under optimized conditions (Table 1), coupled with the ultrafast PET time constant of 0.3 ps (Figure 2d), highlighting the efficiency of the reaction and the role of the micellar environment.
