Executive Summary
This paper investigates the initial-value problem for a class of nonlocal, nonlinear coupled wave equations, specifically those involving convolution operators with nonnegative Fourier transforms. The motivation stems from the applicability of these equations to models in elasticity and quasi-continuum approximations. The study establishes local existence, sufficient conditions for finite-time blow-up, and global existence of solutions. The core of the analysis involves the Cauchy problem for a system of two nonlocal nonlinear wave equations (1.1-1.4). The authors prove local well-posedness for initial data in Sobolev spaces Hs with s > 1/2 and r1, r2 ≥ 2 (Theorem 2.4). They then derive conditions for finite-time blow-up (Theorem 4.2) based on the energy of the system and properties of the nonlinear terms. Furthermore, the paper presents two global existence results: one for kernels with smoothness conditions (r1, r2 > 3, Theorem 5.1) and another for mildly singular kernels (Theorem 5.2). The novelty lies in extending the analysis of blow-up and global existence to a coupled system of nonlocal wave equations, with specific results for different kernel function properties. The results are significant because they provide a framework for understanding the long-time behavior of solutions to these types of equations, which are relevant to various physical models. A key limitation is the restriction to specific classes of kernel functions and the dependence on the initial data's properties for global existence.

Key Results
The paper establishes several key results regarding the Cauchy problem (1.1-1.4). Theorem 2.4 proves local well-posedness for initial data in Hs with s > 1/2 and r1, r2 ≥ 2. Theorem 4.2 provides sufficient conditions for finite-time blow-up. Specifically, if P1ϕ1, P2ϕ2, P1ψ1, P2ψ2 ∈ L2 and G(ϕ1, ϕ2) ∈ L1, and if there is some ν > 0 such that u1f1(u1, u2) + u2f2(u1, u2) ≤ 2(1 + 2ν)F(u1, u2) and E(0) < 0, then the solution blows up in finite time. Theorem 5.1 proves global existence for kernels with smoothness conditions (r1, r2 > 3).
