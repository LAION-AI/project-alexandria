Executive Summary
This study investigated the influence of *Enterococcus faecalis* (E. faecalis) virulence factors, specifically Fsr, GelE, and SprE, on the immune response of *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit flies) and its impact on survival. The research demonstrated that while drosomycin production and phagocytosis were not significantly affected by the presence of these virulence factors, melanization was altered. Specifically, pre-pupae infected with the wild-type E. faecalis strain (V583) exhibited increased melanization and reduced survival compared to those infected with a mutant strain lacking Fsr, GelE, and SprE (V583ΔfsrBΔgelEΔsprE). Further, the study revealed that the impact of melanization on fly survival depended on the E. faecalis strain. Flies with a mutation in prophenoloxidases (PPO1 and PPO2), which impairs melanization, showed increased tolerance to the mutant E. faecalis strain. These findings suggest that the E. faecalis virulence factors modulate the effect of melanization on Drosophila survival. The study highlights the importance of considering pathogen genomic content when studying host-pathogen interactions and suggests that the balance between melanization and the presence of virulence factors influences the outcome of infection. The main limitation is the focus on a specific pathogen and host, which may limit generalizability. The study's significance lies in its insights into the complex interplay between pathogen virulence and host immune responses, potentially informing strategies to combat opportunistic infections.

Research Context
Opportunistic pathogens like *E. faecalis* pose a significant threat, particularly in individuals with compromised immune systems. The *fsr* operon in *E. faecalis* regulates virulence factors, including GelE and SprE, which are known to cause mortality in *Drosophila melanogaster*. Prior research has established the role of these virulence factors in fly mortality. However, the precise mechanisms by which these factors interact with the host's immune response, especially melanization, remained unclear. This study addresses the gap in understanding the specific effects of Fsr, GelE, and SprE on the host's immune response, specifically focusing on melanization, and how it influences survival outcomes. Prior studies have investigated the role of AMPs and phagocytosis, but the role of melanization in the context of these virulence factors was not fully explored.

Methodological Details
The study employed an experimental design involving the infection of *Drosophila melanogaster* with different strains of *E. faecalis*. The study used two *E. faecalis* strains: wild-type V583 and a mutant strain V583ΔfsrBΔgelEΔsprE lacking the *fsr, gelE,* and *sprE* genes. Additionally, the study used *M. luteus* as a control strain. Two *Drosophila melanogaster* lines were used: wild-type W1118 and a mutant line PPO1ΔPPO2Δ, deficient in prophenoloxidases. Male flies were used in the survival experiments. Flies were injected with 50 nl of bacteria at an OD (600 nm) of 0.02 using a nano-injector. The injection procedure involved anesthetizing the flies with CO2 and injecting the bacteria using a pulled glass capillary needle. The injected flies were placed at 29°C with 65% humidity. Survival was assessed by monitoring the flies at 0, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 24 hours post-injection. For the melanization experiments, pre-pupae were injected with the bacterial strains and observed at 0, 6, 24, and 48 hours post-injection using a stereoscope. The number of bacteria injected at time zero was determined to measure reproducibility. The survival experiments involved 75 flies per strain, with each experiment repeated three times. Statistical analysis of Drosophila survival was performed using Log-rank and Gehan-Breslow-Wilcoxon tests and Student's t-test using GraphPad Prism software version 5.03. The study did not mention preregistration or multiple-comparison corrections.

Key Results
The study found that the expression of drosomycin, an antimicrobial peptide, was induced to similar levels by both the wild-type and mutant *E. faecalis* strains (Figure 1). The study also found that the survival rates of flies without hemocytes were only slightly increased when infected with the wild-type strain (Figure 2A) and the mutant strain (Figure 2B). Pre-pupae infected with the wild-type strain showed exacerbated melanization, whereas melanization remained restricted to the wound site in pre-pupae infected with the mutant strain (Figure 3). The pre-pupae infected with the wild-type bacteria died after 24 hours, while those infected with the mutant bacteria were still alive after 48 hours. When comparing PPO mutant and control flies infected with the wild-type bacteria, survival rates were similar (Figure 4). However, in the first 12 hours, PPO1ΔPPO2Δ flies were affected in their capacity to survive infection by the wild-type strain. 24 hours after infection, 80% of the PPO1ΔPPO2Δ mutant flies infected with the triple mutant were still alive, in contrast to the wild-type flies, where all were dead (Figure 4B).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that the virulence factors Fsr, GelE, and SprE in *E. faecalis* modulate the melanization response in *Drosophila melanogaster*, impacting fly survival. The study implies that the presence of these virulence factors leads to increased melanization, which may contribute to host death. The results also indicate that the outcome of infection depends on the interplay between the pathogen's genomic content and the host's immune status, particularly PO activity. The findings suggest that the bacterial proteases may induce tissue injury, leading to overwhelming melanization. The study highlights the complexity of host-pathogen interactions and emphasizes the need to consider the pathogen's genomic content when studying immune responses. The results suggest that melanization can have both positive and negative effects on the host, depending on the presence of virulence factors.

Three Takeaways
The study's core novel contribution is the demonstration that the *E. faecalis* virulence factors Fsr, GelE, and SprE modulate the melanization response in *Drosophila melanogaster*, impacting fly survival. The strongest evidence is that pre-pupae infected with the wild-type *E. faecalis* strain (V583) showed increased melanization and reduced survival, while those infected with the mutant strain (V583ΔfsrBΔgelEΔsprE) exhibited restricted melanization and increased survival (Figure 3). The primary limitation is the focus on a specific host-pathogen interaction, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other systems. Future studies should explore the mechanisms by which these virulence factors influence melanization and the broader implications for host immunity.
