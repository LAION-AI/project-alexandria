Executive Summary
This study investigated the phenomenon of spontaneous regression of micro-metastases following primary tumor (PT) excision in a mouse model of breast cancer. The research team used MDA-MB-231 HM human breast cancer cells, engineered to express luciferase, to track early-stage metastasis in BALB/c nu/nu mice. The removal of the PT led to a significant regression of micro-metastases, but not larger metastases. Supplementation with tumor secretome diminished this regression, suggesting that factors secreted by the PT promote early metastatic growth. In vitro experiments showed that cancer cell-conditioned medium reduced apoptosis and enhanced cell adhesion. Cytokine array and proteomic analyses of the MDA-MB-231 HM secretome identified factors involved in angiogenesis, growth factor activity, focal adhesion, and apoptosis regulation. In vivo blockade of four key secreted factors (IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF) arrested micro-metastasis development in the presence of the PT. High levels of these four factors correlated with poor survival in a lung adenocarcinoma patient cohort from the TCGA dataset. The study's novelty lies in the establishment of an animal model demonstrating metastatic regression following PT removal and identifying specific secreted factors that mediate this process. The findings suggest that the PT secretome plays a crucial role in early metastatic stages and could inform novel approaches to control minimal residual disease during and after PT excision. A key limitation is the use of a single cancer cell line and the need for further validation in other models.

Research Context
Surgical removal of the primary tumor is a standard cancer treatment, yet it has been suggested to potentially accelerate postoperative progression of minimal residual disease. Conversely, rare cases of spontaneous regression of metastases following PT excision have been documented, particularly in breast and lung cancers. Prior research has proposed various mechanisms for this regression, including surgical trauma and the elimination of stimulating factors secreted by the PT. However, these mechanisms have not been empirically tested due to the lack of an animal model demonstrating spontaneous regression. This study addresses this gap by creating a model to investigate the interactions between the PT and metastases, focusing on the role of the PT secretome in early metastatic development.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research question is: What is the role of the primary tumor secretome in the regression of micro-metastases following primary tumor excision?

The primary hypothesis is that the PT secretome supports the survival and growth of distant micro-metastases, and that its elimination by surgery results in metastatic regression. Alternative hypotheses include that the surgical procedure itself, rather than the removal of the tumor mass, elicits metastatic regression. The study predicts that removing the PT will lead to regression of micro-metastases, which will be reversed by supplementing the tumor secretome. The study also predicts that blocking specific factors secreted by the PT will inhibit metastatic progression. This study differs from prior work by providing an empirical model to test the role of the PT secretome in metastatic regression, whereas previous studies have been limited to case reports and correlational analyses.

Methodological Details
The study employed an in vivo model using female BALB/c nu/nu mice. MDA-MB-231 HM human breast cancer cells, engineered to express luciferase, were injected into the fourth mammary fat pad to form a primary tumor. Primary tumor volume was calculated using the formula: (length × width²)×0.5. Metastases were assessed by bioluminescence imaging using an IVIS spectrum apparatus following i.p. injection of 150 mg/kg D-luciferin. The primary tumor was excised when metastatic foci reached a total count of 10⁶ photons/s. Complete removal was verified by bioluminescence imaging. Sham surgery involved an identical incision without tumor removal. Tumor cell-conditioned medium (CM) was prepared by incubating MDA-MB-231 HM cells in serum-free medium for 24 h. For in vivo supplementation, CM was concentrated and delivered via Alzet osmotic minipumps. Apoptosis and adhesion studies were conducted in vitro using CM and serum-free medium (SM). Tube formation assays were performed using human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC). Human cytokine analysis was performed using a Human Cytokine Array. Mass spectrometry-based secretome analysis was conducted on CM samples. Neutralizing antibodies against IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF were used in vivo. Histology was performed on lung sections. Patient outcome analysis was conducted using the TCGA provisional dataset. Statistical analyses included repeated-measures or factorial ANOVA, Student's t-tests, and Fisher's PLSD contrasts. All statistical tests were two-sided, with a predetermined significance level of 0.05. The study was approved by Monash University or Tel-Aviv University Animal Ethics Committees.

Procedures Architectures
The study utilized an orthotopic mouse model of breast cancer metastasis. MDA-MB-231 HM cells, a highly metastatic variant, were engineered to express luciferase for sensitive in vivo imaging of metastases. Mice were injected with cancer cells to develop primary tumors in the mammary fat pad. Bioluminescence imaging was used to monitor the progression of metastases. Primary tumors were surgically excised when metastases reached a specific bioluminescence threshold. Sham surgery served as a control. Tumor cell-conditioned medium (CM) was prepared by culturing MDA-MB-231 HM cells in serum-free medium. CM was then concentrated and used in in vivo experiments via osmotic minipumps, and in vitro studies. A human cytokine array was employed to analyze the secretome, and mass spectrometry was used for proteomic analysis. For in vivo neutralization, mice were injected with a cocktail of neutralizing antibodies against IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF. The study used the IncuCyte system for in vitro cell adhesion and tube formation assays. The TCGA provisional dataset was used for patient outcome analysis. The novelty lies in the use of a luciferase reporter to study early-stage micro-metastases and the in vivo manipulation of the tumor secretome to assess its impact on metastatic progression and regression.

Key Results
The study found that excision of the primary tumor led to a dramatic decrease in metastatic signal, up to 100-fold, within 24 hours (p<0.0001). This regression was more pronounced in smaller metastases. Mice undergoing sham surgery showed continued metastatic growth (p<0.05). Supplementation of the tumor secretome via osmotic mini-pumps reduced the regression by 10-fold (p<0.005). In vitro, CM enhanced cancer cell viability (p<0.001), adhesion (p<0.01), and induced tube formation of human endothelial cells (p<0.0001). Cytokine array and proteomic analyses identified four key factors: IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF. In vivo antibody blockade of these four factors completely blocked metastatic progression (p<0.0001). High protein levels of these four factors were associated with significantly lower survival in a lung adenocarcinoma cohort (hazard ratio = 2.05, p<0.05).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that the PT secretome plays a crucial role in supporting the survival and progression of early-stage micro-metastases. The study indicates that the removal of the PT, and therefore its secreted factors, induces the regression of these early metastases. The identification of IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF as key mediators provides mechanistic insight into the process. These factors are known to promote angiogenesis and/or suppress apoptosis. The correlation between high levels of these factors and poor survival in lung adenocarcinoma patients supports the clinical relevance of the findings. The study implies that the perioperative period presents an opportunity to control minimal residual disease by targeting these pro-metastatic factors.

Contradictions Limitations
A potential contradiction is that while the study highlights the importance of the PT secretome, it also acknowledges that other mechanisms, such as surgical trauma, could play a role. The study is limited by the use of a single cancer cell line (MDA-MB-231 HM), which may not fully represent the complexity of metastatic processes in other cancer types. The study does not address the potential role of the immune system in metastatic regression. The study does not report the random seed used for the experiments. The generalizability of the findings to other cancer types and patient populations is limited. The study acknowledges that the prevalence of post-operative regression of micro-metastases in cancer patients is currently unknown due to technical limitations.

Robustness Ablation Notes
The study performed a sham surgery control to distinguish between effects of the surgical procedure and the removal of the tumor mass. The study also compared the regression of small versus large metastases.

Ethical Considerations
All procedures were approved by Monash University or Tel-Aviv University Animal Ethics Committees. The study used mice and human cell lines.

Three Takeaways
(1) The core novel contribution is the demonstration of metastatic regression following primary tumor excision in an animal model and the identification of specific factors secreted by the primary tumor that mediate this process.

(2) The strongest evidence is that excision of the primary tumor led to a dramatic decrease in metastatic signal, up to 100-fold, within 24 hours (p<0.0001), and that in vivo blockade of four key secreted factors (IL-8, PDGFaa, Serpin E1, and MIF) completely blocked metastatic progression (p<0.0001).

(3) The primary limitation is the use of a single cancer cell line, which limits the generalizability of the findings. Future research should investigate the role of these factors in other cancer models and explore potential therapeutic strategies targeting these factors to control minimal residual disease.
