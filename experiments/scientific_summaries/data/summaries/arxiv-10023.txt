Research Context
The Dirac equation, a cornerstone of relativistic quantum mechanics, accurately describes the electron's properties, including its gyromagnetic ratio (g=2). However, experimental measurements reveal an anomalous gyromagnetic ratio for the electron and other spin-1/2 particles, deviating from the Dirac equation's prediction. This discrepancy has led to modifications of the Dirac equation and the development of Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) to account for these anomalies. QED, while highly accurate, relies on methods like renormalization, which the author views as unnatural. The author seeks an alternative explanation for the anomalous gyromagnetic ratio by considering the effects of curved spacetime, motivated by the General Theory of Relativity, which posits that matter curves spacetime. Prior approaches, such as QED, treat particles as point-like, whereas this work proposes that particles have a finite size.
