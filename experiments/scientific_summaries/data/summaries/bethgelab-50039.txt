Executive Summary
This study investigated the insecticidal potential of *Ajuga iva* (A. iva) extracts against the African cotton leafworm, *Spodoptera littoralis* (*S. littoralis*), a major agricultural pest. The research aimed to determine if A. iva crude leaf extract and its isolated phytoecdysteroids affect larval development and survival. The study employed a series of experiments where *S. littoralis* larvae were exposed to different concentrations of A. iva crude leaf extract and a mixture of purified phytoecdysteroids. Results showed that the A. iva crude leaf extract significantly increased mortality in first instar larvae (up to 85% at 250 µg/µl) and reduced the relative growth rate (RGR) and larval weight gain (LWG) in third instar larvae. The highest concentration of crude extract (250 µg/µl) and the purified phytoecdysteroid mixture also led to reduced survival and inhibited pupation. The study also examined the gut morphology of treated larvae, revealing disrupted actin filaments and abnormal nuclei. The study's novelty lies in demonstrating the efficacy of A. iva extracts against *S. littoralis*, supporting the potential of phytoecdysteroids for pest control. A limitation is the lack of fractionation of clerodanes from the A. iva crude leaf extract due to the difficulty in calibrating the protocol and the high cost of standards.

Research Context
Insecticides pose risks to human health and the environment, prompting interest in plant-derived alternatives. Phytoecdysteroids, found in some plants, are analogs of insect hormones and can disrupt insect development. Prior research has shown that phytoecdysteroids from *A. iva* have activity against other pests. This study builds on previous research by Taha-Salaime et al. (2019) that identified phytoecdysteroids and clerodanes in *A. iva*. Prior work lacked a direct investigation of the effects of *A. iva* extracts on *S. littoralis* larvae, a major agricultural pest.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research questions were: (1) Does *A. iva* crude leaf extract affect *S. littoralis* larvae? (2) Do phytoecdysteroids isolated from the crude leaf extract and commercial standards have different effects on the larvae? (3) Do phytoecdysteroids have a direct effect on the larvae's gut? The main hypothesis was that crude extract of *A. iva* leaves, containing phytoecdysteroids, would reduce the damage caused by *S. littoralis* larvae. Alternative hypotheses included that the extract would have no effect, or that the phytoecdysteroids would have different effects than the crude extract. The study differed from previous work by focusing on *S. littoralis* and by examining the effects of the extract on larval gut morphology.

Methodological Details
The study used a controlled experimental design to assess the effects of *A. iva* extracts on *S. littoralis* larvae. First instar larvae (n=11 replicates, 10 larvae/replicate) were exposed to castor bean leaves treated with three concentrations (50, 100, and 250 µg/µl) of *A. iva* crude leaf extract. Mortality was assessed after 3 days. Third instar larvae were exposed to four treatments: (1) 250 µg/µl *A. iva* crude leaf extract for 4 days, then a freshly treated leaf for 4 more days; (2) the same treatment as (1) with 250 µg/µl of a fractionated mixture of three phytoecdysteroids from *A. iva* leaf extract; (3) *A. iva* crude leaf extract for 4 days, and then a control castor bean leaf treated with ddH2O for the next 4 days; (4) a control castor bean leaf for 4 days and then *A. iva* crude leaf extract for the next 4 days. Larval survival, RGR, and LWG were measured. Gut morphology was examined using DAPI and phalloidin staining. The *A. iva* crude extracts were prepared by soaking leaf and stem powder in methanol, homogenizing, centrifuging, filtering, and concentrating under vacuum. Phytoecdysteroids were identified using LC-TOF-MS and TLC. Statistical analyses included t-tests, mixed-model ANOVA, and Friedman tests. Significance was set at p < 0.05. The study was not preregistered, and no information about IRB or ethical considerations was provided.

Procedures Architectures
The study involved the extraction and purification of phytoecdysteroids from *A. iva* leaves and stems. The extraction process involved soaking the plant material in methanol, homogenization, centrifugation, filtration, and vacuum concentration. The resulting extract was analyzed using LC-TOF-MS. For purification, the extract was treated with water to give 30% aqueous methanol, and then extracted as described in Aly et al. (2011). TLC was used to separate the components. The biological activity tests involved smearing castor bean leaves with the *A. iva* crude leaf extract or purified phytoecdysteroid mixture. First instar larvae were placed on the treated leaves, and mortality was assessed. Third instar larvae were exposed to treated leaves for varying durations, and larval survival, RGR, and LWG were measured. Gut morphology was examined using DAPI and phalloidin staining under a Leica confocal microscope. The study used a paint brush to apply the extract to the leaves. The study is new in its application of *A. iva* extracts to *S. littoralis* and its investigation of the effects on larval gut morphology.

Key Results
First instar larvae showed significantly increased mortality after feeding on *A. iva* crude leaf extract: 25%, 65%, and 85% for 50, 100, and 250 µg/µl concentrations, respectively, compared to 5% in the control (p < 0.001). Third instar larvae fed on crude leaf extract showed reduced LWG (F3.104 = 20.334, 17.246 and 13.007, p < 0.001) compared to the control. All concentrations of crude leaf extract significantly decreased larval RGR compared to the control (p < 0.001). With the highest concentration of crude leaf extract, RGR decreased by 0.05 and 0.20 g/g day on days 6 and 8, respectively, compared to the control (F3.16, 18 =12.641, p < 0.001). All concentrations of *A. iva* crude leaf extract significantly reduced third instar larval survival after 11 days (X2 3 = 6.221, p = 0.038). Larvae fed on 250 µg/µl crude leaf extract or the phytoecdysteroid mixture lost weight, stopped growing, and died. Larval guts showed disrupted actin filaments and abnormal nuclei in larvae treated with 250 µg/µl crude leaf extract or the phytoecdysteroid mixture.

Interpretation Implications
The findings support the hypothesis that *A. iva* extracts, particularly the phytoecdysteroids, negatively affect *S. littoralis* development and survival. The results suggest that phytoecdysteroids interfere with the insect's developmental stages, especially during metamorphosis, potentially by disrupting hormonal balance. The observed gut morphology changes, including disrupted actin filaments and abnormal nuclei, indicate a possible mechanism of action. The study suggests that the phytoecdysteroids in *A. iva* may be useful for the management of economically important insect pests.

Contradictions Limitations
A key limitation is the lack of fractionation of clerodanes from the *A. iva* crude leaf extract. The study did not include commercial clerodane standards in the treatments. The study does not report the random seed, train/test split, or demographics. The study does not mention any ethical considerations or IRB approval. The study acknowledges that the phytoecdysteroids may affect insects by interfering with their developmental stages, especially during metamorphosis. The study also mentions that the larval gut cells showed histolysis with clear signs of apoptosis.

Three Takeaways
(1) The study demonstrates that *A. iva* crude leaf extract and its isolated phytoecdysteroids exhibit insecticidal activity against *S. littoralis*, a significant agricultural pest. (2) The strongest evidence is the dose-dependent increase in mortality of first instar larvae (up to 85%) and the reduction in RGR and survival of third instar larvae, along with the observed gut morphology changes. (3) A primary limitation is the lack of fractionation of clerodanes from the *A. iva* crude leaf extract, which could have provided further insights into the extract's activity. Future research could investigate the role of clerodanes and other compounds in the extract's insecticidal effects.
