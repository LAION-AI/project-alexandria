Executive Summary
This study synthesized 25 tricyclic 2-phenoxypyrido[3,2-e][1,2,4]triazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidin-5(4H)-ones hybrids and evaluated their α-glucosidase inhibitory activity to address the need for new antidiabetic agents. Compounds 6h and 6d exhibited stronger activity than acarbose, with compound 6h showing the highest inhibition (IC50 = 104.07 µM). Molecular modeling revealed that compound 6h inhibits α-glucosidase by forming a stable ligand-α-glucosidase complex, with extra hydrogen bond interactions directed by Trp329 in the binding site. The study synthesized a series of compounds and tested their inhibitory activity, finding that the amino-bridge substitution of the pyrimidine ring at position 4 is responsible for the strong inhibitory activity. The study's results suggest a new lead scaffold for developing new clinically effective antidiabetic drugs. Limitations include the lack of information on the synthesis of the starting material (A) and the absence of a detailed discussion of the gastrointestinal side effects. The study provides a new structural insight for the development of novel α-glucosidase inhibitors that differ from commonly employed carbasugars, potentially leading to new clinical drugs for the treatment of type II diabetes.

Research Context
The increasing prevalence of diabetes necessitates the development of new antidiabetic agents. Existing α-glucosidase inhibitors, such as acarbose, are sugar mimetics, which can cause gastrointestinal side effects, reducing patient compliance. Prior work has focused on sugar mimetics, which have limitations. This study aims to overcome these limitations by exploring and developing α-glucosidase inhibitors based on modified scaffolds. The study builds on previous research on the pharmacological activities of pyridine, triazole, and pyrimidine scaffolds, which have been incorporated into compounds with antimicrobial, antiviral, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anticonvulsant activities.

Methodological Details
The study employed an in vitro screening of α-glucosidase inhibition. The study synthesized 25 tricyclic 2-phenoxypyrido[3,2-e][1,2,4]triazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidin-5(4H)-ones hybrids. The starting material (A) was prepared according to a reported procedure. The compounds were screened at a concentration of 100 µg/mL. The test was repeated three times for each compound. The IC50 values were calculated for compounds that exhibited average percent inhibition of more than 50%. Acarbose was used as a positive control. Molecular docking calculations were performed using the Autodock software package. The original docked protein was downloaded from the RCSB data bank web site (PDB file with code 3W37). The α-glucosidase inhibitory activity was determined based on the described method by Ahmad et al., 2011. The percent inhibition (%) of α-glucosidase inhibitory activity was calculated using a formula. The study does not mention preregistration or multiple-comparison corrections. The study used phosphate-buffered saline pH 6.5, prepared by dissolving sodium phosphate monobasic, sodium chloride, potassium chloride and potassium dihydrogen phosphate in deionized water. A 20-mM solution of para-nitrophenyl-α-D-glucopyranoside (p-NPG substrate) was prepared by dissolving 6 mg of p-NPG in 10 mL of buffer solution. The α-glucosidase enzyme solution was prepared by dissolving α-glucosidase enzyme type 1 from Baker's yeast (1 mg) in cold phosphate-buffered saline (1000 µL), pH 6.5. For the screening of samples, 200 µg/mL concentration was prepared in 100% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). Then, two-fold serial dilutions with 5% DMSO were prepared in a 96-well microplate to give final concentrations of 100, 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, 3.13 and 1.56 µg/ml.

Procedures Architectures
The study synthesized 25 tricyclic compounds. The synthetic route towards fused pyrimidine derivatives is illustrated in Figure 2. The starting material (A) was prepared according to a reported procedure. The 1,2,4-triazole intermediate B was obtained when an ethanolic solution of diphenoxy-Ncyanoimidocarbonate was allowed to react with A in a basic medium (triethylamine) at room temperature. The reaction of B with conc. HCl afforded [1,2,4]triazolo [1,5-a]pyrimidin-5(4H)-ones (2a-l). Compounds 2a-l were obtained as coloured amorphous powders. 2-phenoxypyrido [3,2-e] [1,2,4]triazolo [1,5-a]pyrimidin-5(4H)-thione (3) was obtained as a yellow amorphous powder. The conversion of 3 into 4 was carried out in basic medium using benzyl bromide. The transformation of 1 into 5 was successfully achieved employing phosphorous oxychloride as chlorinating agent in boiling benzene followed by treatment with a saturated basic solution. Reaction of 5 with hydroxylamine HCl provided the corresponding product 6e. Treatment of target 5 with several aromatic amines afforded the corresponding compounds 6a-d. The chlorine was replaced in 5 by isoniazid to give amidrazone 6i. Hydrazinolysis of 5 produced the corresponding compound 6f, which upon treatment with equimolar amounts of aldehydes resulted in the formation of hydrazone derivatives 6g and 6h. The α-glucosidase inhibitory assay involved pre-incubating the enzyme with the compounds at 37°C for 10 minutes, followed by the addition of p-NPG solution and measuring absorbance at 405 nm. Molecular docking calculations were performed using the Autodock software package. The study used a 96-well microplate for the screening of samples.

Key Results
Compounds 6h and 6d showed stronger α-glucosidase inhibitory activity than acarbose. Compound 6h exhibited the highest inhibition with an IC50 of 104.07 µM. Compounds 3, 6c, and 6d showed an average percent inhibition of more than 60%. Compounds 2a, 2d, 6a, 6b, and 6h showed an average percent inhibition of more than 50% but less than 60%. The IC50 of compound 3 was 167.82 µM, and those of compounds 2a and 2d were 304.13 µM and 173.83 µM, respectively. The binding energy of docked 6h was found to be the lowest (-10.56 kcal/mol). Compound 6a showed a higher inhibitory activity than 6b, with a binding energy of -9.76 kcal/mol compared to -9.17 kcal/mol for 6b. Acarbose can establish 10 hydrogen bonds. Compound 6h can establish three hydrogen bonds. The average percent inhibitions of 2a and 2d were 50.36% and 57.48%, respectively. Compound 3 at a concentration of 100 µg/mL exhibited good activity against α-glucosidase enzyme (60.38%).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that the synthesized tricyclic compounds, particularly 6h and 6d, are promising α-glucosidase inhibitors. The lower IC50 values of 6h and 6d compared to acarbose indicate potential for improved efficacy. The molecular docking results suggest that the binding energy and the stability of the ligand-α-glucosidase complex are responsible for the inhibitory activity of compound 6h. The role of Trp329 in directing the ligand in the binding site is also significant. The study provides a new structural insight for the development of novel α-glucosidase inhibitors, which differ from the commonly employed carbasugars. The results support the hypothesis that the amino-bridge substitution of the pyrimidine ring at position 4 is responsible for the strong inhibitory activity.
