Executive Summary
This paper presents a comparative analysis of various image denoising algorithms, focusing on their performance in removing noise from grayscale images. The study categorizes noise types into additive (e.g., Gaussian) and multiplicative (e.g., speckle) categories, and examines spatial filtering (linear and non-linear) and transform domain filtering (spatial-frequency and wavelet domain) techniques. The performance of different denoising algorithms was evaluated using Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Image Enhancement Factor (IEF) metrics. The algorithms were tested on a grayscale Lena image corrupted with 90% noise density. The results, as presented in Table I, show that the Modified Decision Based Un-symmetric Trimmed Median Filter (MDBUTMF) achieved the highest PSNR (17.9865) and IEF (16.2066) values, indicating superior denoising performance. The study highlights the importance of selecting an appropriate denoising algorithm based on the type of noise present in the image. A key limitation is the exclusive focus on a single test image (Lena) and a specific noise density, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research directions include exploring more advanced denoising techniques and evaluating their performance across diverse image datasets and noise conditions.

Key Results
The study's key results are presented in Table I, which compares the performance of different image denoising filters. The table includes the following results for each filter: Mean Filter (MF): PSNR = 6.5759, IEF = 1.1712; Adaptive Mean Filter (AMF): PSNR = 8.0603, IEF = 1.6499; Progressive Switching Median Filter (PSMF): PSNR = 6.7847, IEF = 1.2257; Decision Based Algorithm (DBA): PSNR = 17.1205, IEF = 13.2768; Modified Decision Based Algorithm (MDBA): PSNR = 17.2242, IEF = 13.5976; Modified Decision Based Un-symmetric Trimmed Median Filter (MDBUTMF): PSNR = 17.9865, IEF = 16.2066. The MDBUTMF achieved the highest PSNR and IEF values, indicating superior denoising performance. The DBA and MDBA also performed well, but not as well as the MDBUTMF. The other filters (MF, AMF, PSMF) showed significantly lower PSNR and IEF values. No information on effect sizes, confidence intervals, or p-values is provided.
