Executive Summary
This paper challenges the traditional understanding of phonon mode behavior in semiconductor alloys, specifically those with substitutional disorder. The study demonstrates that seemingly disparate alloy systems, such as InGaAs, InGaP, and ZnTeSe, actually exhibit a universal phonon mode behavior of a percolation-type (1-bond→2-mode). This contrasts with the virtual crystal approximation (VCA), which leads to a 1-bond→1-mode behavior. The authors propose a shift from the VCA's macroscopic perspective to a mesoscopic, percolation-based model to better understand alloy disorder. The research is supported by ab initio simulations of the phonon density of states at the zone-center of representative supercells and a simple ab initio protocol is proposed to estimate the parameters of the percolation model. The key finding is the unification of the phonon mode behavior across different alloy systems, suggesting a more fundamental understanding of vibrational spectra in these materials. The study's novelty lies in its mesoscopic perspective on alloy disorder, offering a new framework for interpreting vibrational spectroscopy data. The main limitation is the reliance on a simplified model and the need for further validation across a wider range of alloy compositions and systems.

Research Context
The traditional approach to understanding vibrational spectra in semiconductor alloys relies on the virtual crystal approximation (VCA), which simplifies the alloy disorder at a macroscopic scale. The VCA, however, struggles to accurately classify and explain the phonon mode behavior in certain alloy systems, particularly those exhibiting mixed-mode or 2-bond→1-mode behavior. Prior work, such as the modified-random-element-isodisplacement (MREI) model and the Elliott-CPA criterion, have been used to classify the TO-Raman/IR data. These approaches, however, have limitations, particularly in explaining the behavior of alloys like InGaP. This work addresses the gap by proposing a percolation-based model that accounts for the mesoscopic scale of alloy disorder, offering a more nuanced understanding of phonon behavior.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research question is whether different semiconductor alloys, traditionally classified with different phonon mode behaviors, can be unified under a single, universal model. The primary hypothesis is that alloys like InGaAs (1-bond→1-mode), InGaP (modified 2-mode), and ZnTeSe (2-bond→1-mode) share a common phonon mode behavior, specifically a percolation-type (1-bond→2-mode). The alternative hypothesis is that the observed differences in phonon behavior are fundamental and cannot be explained by a unified model. The study aims to support the primary hypothesis by demonstrating the applicability of a percolation model to ZnTeSe, which would unify its behavior with that of InGaAs and InGaP. This approach differs from prior work by shifting from a macroscopic to a mesoscopic perspective, focusing on the topology of the substituting species rather than averaging properties.

Methodological Details
The study employs a combination of theoretical modeling and ab initio calculations. The core methodology involves the development and application of a percolation model to describe the phonon mode behavior in semiconductor alloys. The model is applied to three representative systems: InGaAs, InGaP, and ZnTeSe. The study uses the virtual crystal approximation (VCA) and the modified-random-element-isodisplacement (MREI) model as baselines for comparison. The ab initio calculations are performed using the plane-wave pseudopotential method within the density functional theory and the local density approximation (LDA). The calculations are performed using the computer code Siesta. The study focuses on the transverse optical (TO) modes, which are probed by Raman and Infrared (IR) spectroscopies. The study uses 64-atom supercells for the ab initio calculations, which are well-converged in size. The study also proposes a simple ab initio protocol to estimate the parameters of the percolation model. The study analyzes the TO density of states (TO-DOS) at the zone-center (ZC) to compare with the Raman signal. The study uses the Grüneisen parameter (γ) to estimate the impurity frequency. Procedures Architectures
The study utilizes a percolation model to describe the phonon mode behavior in semiconductor alloys. The percolation model distinguishes between vibrations of like bonds within B-rich and C-rich regions, resulting from different local bond distortions. The model predicts a splitting of each MREI-like TO branch into a symmetrical double-branch, attached to the parent and impurity modes. The study also employs ab initio calculations using the plane-wave pseudopotential method within the density functional theory and the local density approximation (LDA). The calculations are performed using the computer code Siesta. The ab initio protocol involves calculating the impurity frequency (ωimp) and the splitting (Δ) using supercells. The study uses 64-atom supercells for the ab initio calculations. To access ωimp, a supercell containing a single impurity (∼3% imp.) is used. To access Δ, a supercell containing two neighboring substitutional impurities is used. The study proposes a three-oscillator [1 Zn-Te, 2 Zn-Se] version of the percolation model for ZnTeSe. The model's architecture involves the calculation of TO and LO Raman lineshapes using a classical form of the dielectric function, generalized to three harmonic oscillators. The study uses small dampings for the Zn-Te (1 cm−1) and Zn-Se (3 cm−1) modes.

Key Results
The study demonstrates that the 1-bond→2-phonon percolation model applies to ZnTeSe, as it does to InGaAs and InGaP. The authors propose a three-oscillator version for ZnTeSe, supported by existing EXAFS data and ab initio phonon calculations. The agreement between the theoretical curves and the data is rather good (Figure 2). The study shows that the percolation schemes are consistent with the MREI-VCA classification. The study finds that the Zn-Te and Zn-Se bonds have (ωimp, Δ) values of (∼189 cm−1, ∼0 cm−1) and (∼195 cm−1, ∼8 cm−1), respectively. The ab initio calculations show three equidistant oscillators spaced by ∼10 cm−1 in the ZC TO-DOS (Figure 3). The study finds that the interplay between the frequencies and strengths of the three oscillators in the ZC TO-DOS and the phenomenological TO Raman lineshapes are consistent over the whole composition domain. The study shows a slight overall blue-shift of the ZC TO-DOS with respect to the corresponding Raman lineshape (∼20 cm−1).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest a unified understanding of phonon mode behavior in semiconductor alloys, challenging the traditional classification based on the virtual crystal approximation (VCA). The percolation model provides a mesoscopic perspective on alloy disorder, offering a more accurate description of vibrational spectra. The results imply that the seemingly disparate behaviors of InGaAs, InGaP, and ZnTeSe can be explained by a single underlying mechanism. The study's findings suggest that vibrational spectroscopies can provide insight into alloy disorder at the mesoscopic scale. The study emphasizes the importance of considering the TO modes and their behavior in alloys, as LO modes can obscure the underlying 1-bond→2-mode behavior. The study's results suggest that the traditional MREI-VCA/Elliott-CPA classification can be unified into a single class covered by the percolation model.

Contradictions Limitations
The study relies on a simplified percolation model, and the generalizability of the model to other alloy systems is not explicitly addressed. The study does not provide information about the specific composition ranges or experimental conditions used for the Raman/IR data. The study acknowledges a slight discrepancy with respect to the TOTeZn−Se branch in the Te-dilute limit. The study does not address the limitations of the LDA approximation used in the ab initio calculations. The study does not discuss the potential impact of temperature on the phonon behavior. The study does not mention the random seed used for the simulations. The study does not discuss the limitations of the ab initio protocol.

Three Takeaways
(1) The core novel contribution is the demonstration that the phonon mode behavior in seemingly disparate semiconductor alloys can be unified under a single percolation-type model, offering a new perspective on alloy disorder. (2) The strongest evidence comes from the successful application of the percolation model to ZnTeSe, supported by ab initio calculations and existing experimental data, which provides a more accurate description of the TO mode behavior. (3) The primary limitation is the reliance on a simplified model and the need for further validation across a wider range of alloy compositions and systems. A forward path includes extending the model to other alloy systems and refining the ab initio protocol for improved accuracy.
