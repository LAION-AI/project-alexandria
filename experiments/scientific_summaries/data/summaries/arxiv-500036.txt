Executive Summary
This paper investigates the characterization of Hardy spaces associated with Hermite operators using Lusin area integrals. The Hermite operator, denoted as L, is defined as the negative Laplacian plus the squared Euclidean norm of the position vector. The study focuses on Hardy spaces, denoted as H<sup>p</sup><sub>L</sub>(R<sup>d</sup>), with an index p in the range (0, 1], which are constructed using the maximal function of the semigroup generated by the Hermite operator. The central result, Theorem 1.1, establishes an equivalence between the Hardy space H<sup>p</sup><sub>L</sub>(R<sup>d</sup>) and a space defined using Lusin integrals, denoted as H<sup>p</sup><sub>S</sub>(R<sup>d</sup>). The Lusin integral is defined using derivatives of the form ∂<sub>j</sub> + x<sub>j</sub>, which are specific to Hermite operators. The paper also demonstrates the boundedness of Riesz transforms, denoted as R<sup>L</sup><sub>j</sub>, on H<sup>p</sup><sub>L</sub>(R<sup>d</sup>) for all j (Theorem 1.2). The methodology involves defining intermediate spaces and utilizing atom decompositions to characterize the Hardy spaces. The findings provide a new characterization of Hardy spaces associated with Hermite operators, offering a deeper understanding of their structure and properties. A key limitation is the focus on a specific class of operators, which limits the scope of the results. The work is significant because it provides a novel characterization of Hardy spaces, which are fundamental in harmonic analysis, and extends existing results to a more general setting.
