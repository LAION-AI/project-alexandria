Executive Summary
This paper investigates geometrically maximal abelian coverings of the modular curve $X_0(p)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ that are unramified outside cusps, where $p$ is a prime number. Building upon Mazur's result, which identified the Shimura covering $X_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ as the geometrically maximal unramified abelian covering of $X_0(p)$, the authors demonstrate the existence of a geometrically maximal abelian covering $X'_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ that is cyclic of degree $2N_p$, where $N_p = (p-1)/\gcd(p-1, 12)$. The construction of $X'_2(p)$ relies on generalized Dedekind eta functions. The key finding is that the geometrically maximal abelian covering $X'_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ unramified outside cusps has a degree twice that of the Shimura covering. The approach involves constructing a quadratic extension of the function field $\mathbb{Q}(X_2(p))$ using a square root of a modular function. The result is significant because it provides a complete description of the geometrically maximal abelian covering of $X_0(p)$ unramified outside cusps. A key limitation is the reliance on the properties of generalized Dedekind eta functions, which restricts the analysis to modular curves. The work extends our understanding of the arithmetic of modular curves and their coverings.

Research Context
Mazur proved that the Shimura covering $X_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ is the geometrically maximal unramified abelian covering of $X_0(p)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. This paper addresses the gap in understanding the structure of geometrically maximal abelian coverings of modular curves, specifically focusing on those unramified outside cusps. Prior work, such as that of Mazur, established the foundation for understanding unramified coverings, but did not fully characterize the maximal abelian extensions. This work builds on Mazur's result by identifying a geometrically maximal covering with a degree twice that of the Shimura covering. The paper utilizes generalized Dedekind eta functions, which are related to Siegel functions, to construct the covering. The limitations of prior work include not fully characterizing the maximal abelian extensions and the lack of a construction method for the maximal covering. This paper addresses these limitations by providing a construction and characterization of a geometrically maximal abelian covering.

Methodological Details
The paper employs a theoretical approach, focusing on the construction and analysis of modular curves and their coverings. The study considers the modular curves $X_0(p)$ and $X_1(p)$ as geometrically integral smooth proper curves over $\mathbb{Q}$. The authors choose a model of $X_1(p)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ such that the cusp at infinity splits completely in a finite cyclic covering $f_p: X_1(p) \to X_0(p)$ of degree $(p-1)/2$. The Shimura covering $f_p: X_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ is the unique subcovering of degree $N_p = (p-1)/\gcd(p-1, 12)$. The open subscheme $Y_0(p) \subset X_0(p)$ is defined such that $X_0(p) \setminus Y_0(p)$ consists of two cusps. The function field $\mathbb{Q}(X'_2(p))$ of $X'_2(p)$ is obtained as a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}(X_2(p))$ generated by a square root of an explicitly constructed rational function in $\mathbb{Q}(X_2(p))$. The key ingredient for this construction is the generalized Dedekind eta functions, defined as $E_g(\tau) = q^{NB(g/N)/2} \prod_{m=1}^{\infty} (1 - q^{N(m-1)+g})(1 - q^{Nm-g})$, where $B(x) = x^2 - x + 1/6$ is the second Bernoulli polynomial. The authors use properties of the generalized Dedekind eta functions, including their transformation properties under modular group actions, to construct modular functions on the curves. The proof involves analyzing the Galois module $M_{Tor}$ and its maximal $\mu$-type subgroup $M^{\mu}_{Tor}(-1)$. The analysis relies on the exact sequence $0 \to J^{\mu}_{Tor}(-1) \to M^{\mu}_{Tor}(-1) \to (L_D \otimes \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mu}(-1) \to H^1(\mathbb{Q}, J_{Tor}(-1))$. The paper uses the properties of the generalized Jacobian and the abelian fundamental group of a smooth curve. The study does not mention preregistration or multiple-comparison corrections. Ethical considerations are not discussed.

Procedures Architectures
The paper constructs a geometrically maximal abelian covering $X'_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ of degree $2N_p$. The construction involves defining generalized Dedekind eta functions, denoted as $E_g(\tau)$, where $\tau$ is a complex variable. The functions are defined as $E_g(\tau) = q^{NB(g/N)/2} \prod_{m=1}^{\infty} (1 - q^{N(m-1)+g})(1 - q^{Nm-g})$. The authors then define modular functions $F_h(\tau)$ as products of the generalized Dedekind eta functions. Specifically, for an integer $h$ not congruent to 0 modulo $p$, $F_h(\tau) = \prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1} E_{g^{jk}h}(\tau)^{6/\ell}$, where $\ell = (p-1, 12)/2$. The function $F_h$ is shown to be a modular function on $X_2(p)$. The function field $\mathbb{Q}(X'_2(p))$ is obtained as a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}(X_2(p))$ generated by the square root of $F_h^2$. The modular curve $X'_2(p)$ is associated with the group $\Gamma'_2(p)$, which is the kernel of a character on $\Gamma_2(p)$. The paper uses the properties of the modular group $\Gamma_0(p)$ and its subgroups, such as $\Gamma_1(p)$ and $\Gamma_2(p)$. The key components are the generalized Dedekind eta functions, the modular functions $F_h$, and the modular curves $X_0(p)$, $X_1(p)$, $X_2(p)$, and $X'_2(p)$. The novelty lies in the construction of the geometrically maximal abelian covering $X'_2(p)$ and the use of generalized Dedekind eta functions to achieve this.

Key Results
The paper demonstrates the existence of a geometrically maximal abelian covering $X'_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ of degree $2N_p$. Theorem 1.2.2 states that there exists a cyclic covering $f'_p: X'_2(p) \to X_0(p)$ of degree $2N_p$ such that (1) $f'_p|_{Y'_2(p)}: Y'_2(p) \to Y_0(p)$ is a geometrically maximal unramified covering of $Y_0(p)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, where $Y'_2(p) := f'^{-1}_p(Y_0(p))$; and (2) $f'_p$ factors as $f'_p = f_p \circ \pi_p$, where $\pi_p: X'_2(p) \to X_2(p)$ is a degree two covering that is unramified outside cusps. The proof relies on the properties of the modular functions $F_h$. Lemma 3.3.4 shows that for $\gamma = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma_0(p)$, $F_h(\gamma\tau) = \psi(\gamma)F_{ah}(\tau)$, where $\psi$ is a character of order 2. Furthermore, for $\gamma \in \Gamma_2(p)$, $F_h(\gamma\tau) = \psi(\gamma)\chi(\gamma)F_h(\tau)$ if $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, and $F_h(\gamma\tau) = \psi(\gamma)F_h(\tau)$ if $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$. The order of $F^2_h$ at any cusp of $X_2(p)$ is odd. The order is zero elsewhere. The paper does not provide comparisons to baselines or state any effect sizes or confidence intervals. The results are primarily theoretical, with no negative results reported.

This result contributes to the understanding of the arithmetic of modular curves and their coverings. The construction of $X'_2(p)$ using generalized Dedekind eta functions highlights the importance of these functions in studying the structure of modular curves. The theoretical implications are that the study provides a deeper insight into the Galois theory of modular curves. The results suggest a mechanism where the structure of the maximal abelian extension is closely related to the properties of modular functions. The paper does not discuss the case when $p \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$ in detail. The lack of discussion on computational aspects and the specific properties of the generalized Dedekind eta functions are limitations. The paper does not provide any empirical validation or experimental results. The external validity is limited to the context of modular curves. The paper does not explicitly discuss any conflicts with prior literature, but it builds upon the work of Mazur.
