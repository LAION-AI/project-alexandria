Executive Summary
This study investigated the phylogenetic relationships among the thermophilic bacterial genera *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus* using whole-genome sequencing data. The research aimed to clarify taxonomic boundaries and propose a revised classification. The study analyzed 113 genomes, calculating Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) and Average Amino acid Identity (AAI), and constructing a maximum likelihood tree based on 662 orthologous core genes. The results revealed two main clades: Clade I, containing *Geobacillus*, *Parageobacillus*, and some *Anoxybacillus* species, and Clade II, primarily comprising *Anoxybacillus* species. Clade I was further partitioned into three clades, with the third clade warranting elevation to a new genus, *Quasigeobacillus* gen. nov. The mean ANI and AAI values suggest that the candidate new genus is somewhat more closely related to *Parageobacillus*, followed by *Geobacillus*. The study found that *Anoxybacillus vitaminiphilus* and *Anoxybacillus amylolyticus* showed inconsistent positioning. The research highlights the importance of considering closely related genera together in phylogenetic analyses for accurate taxonomic classification. The primary limitation is the unresolved taxonomic placement of two *Anoxybacillus* species. The study proposes a new genus, *Quasigeobacillus*, and underscores the need for comprehensive phylogenetic assessments in bacterial taxonomy.

Research Context
Prior to this study, bacterial taxonomy relied heavily on DNA-DNA hybridization, and later, 16S rRNA gene sequencing. However, 16S rRNA gene sequencing has limitations due to low resolution. The use of ANI provides higher resolution for detecting relatedness among bacterial genomes. The genera *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus* are thermophilic, spore-forming bacteria belonging to the family *Bacillaceae*. Previous studies have shown the need for taxonomic revisions within these genera, particularly with the reclassification of some *Geobacillus* species into *Parageobacillus*. This study addresses the need for a more comprehensive phylogenetic analysis to clarify the relationships among these genera and refine their classification, as well as the importance of considering closely related genera together when studying phylogeny or assigning genomic affinities.

Methodological Details
The study employed a phylogenomic approach to analyze the relationships among *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus*. The study design involved downloading complete genome assemblies from the RefSeq database. The study included 32 *Anoxybacillus* strains, 73 *Geobacillus* strains, and 8 *Parageobacillus* strains. Additionally, whole-genome sequences of two *Anoxybacillus*, four *Geobacillus*, and one *Parageobacillus* strains were included from a study of thermophiles in Australia. Anomalous genome assemblies were excluded. The core genome was identified using EDGAR 2.0, with the genome assembly of *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* NG80-2 used as a starting reference. Orthologous genes were identified via BLAST. The genome of *Bacillus subtilis* spizizenii TU-B-10T was included as an outgroup. The obtained CDS were concatenated and aligned using MUSCLE. A maximum-likelihood tree was built using FastTree. ANI and AAI values were calculated using the ani.rb and aai.rb scripts from the enveomics package. UPGMA dendrograms were constructed using the DendroUPGMA web server. Trees were plotted with the Interactive Tree Of Life tool. Genes unique to clade I-c were identified using BLASTn. The relaxed BLASTn filtering criteria used were percentage identity 30%, bitscore 10 and percentage overlap of 1%. The study did not specify a preregistration or multiple-comparison corrections. The study adhered to the methods used for reassessment of the genus *Geobacillus* by Aliyu et al. [36].

Procedures Architectures
The study utilized a combination of bioinformatics tools and methods to analyze the genomes of *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus*. The core genome was identified using EDGAR 2.0, which identifies orthologous genes across multiple genomes. The starting reference genome was *Geobacillus thermodenitrificans* NG80-2, and the outgroup was *Bacillus subtilis* spizizenii TU-B-10T. UPGMA dendrograms were generated using the DendroUPGMA web server, which employs the unweighted pair group method with arithmetic mean clustering. Trees were visualized using the Interactive Tree Of Life tool. BLASTn was used to identify genes unique to clade I-c. The study did not involve the creation of new models or architectures; instead, it relied on established bioinformatics tools and methods for phylogenetic analysis. The novelty lies in the application of these methods to a comprehensive dataset of *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus* genomes to refine their taxonomic classification.

Key Results
The core gene maximum likelihood tree, constructed from 662 orthologous genes, revealed three major groups: an outgroup, clade I, and clade II. Clade I contained 87 genomes, and clade II contained 24 *Anoxybacillus* genomes. The two-way ANI values were used to build a UPGMA dendrogram. The two-way average AAI values were used to build a UPGMA dendrogram. The genomes of *Anoxybacillus* sp. EFIL, *Geobacillus* sp. MMMUD3 and *Geobacillus* sp. DSP4a have ANI of 97.99, 97.14 and 97.64 respectively. The genome of *Anoxybacillus* sp. CHMUD had the most distant ANI value with 95.03 and AAI of 95.81. A total of 6 CDS were found to be unique to clade I-c. The mean ANI and AAI values separating the candidate new genus from the members of the existing three genera are shown in Table 1. The mean ANI and AAI values suggest that the candidate new genus is somewhat more closely related to *Parageobacillus*, followed by *Geobacillus*. The structure of the three phylogenetic trees reaffirms the distinctiveness of the two species consistently present in clade I-c with respect to the three existing genera. The study proposes that A. tepidamans and the one genomospecies present in clade I-c should be placed into a new genus called *Quasigeobacillus*. Due to the incongruous positioning of *A. vitaminiphilus* and *A. amylolyticus* the affinities of these two species remains unresolved.

Interpretation Implications
The findings support the need for a revised taxonomic classification of the genera *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus*. The identification of a distinct clade, designated as *Quasigeobacillus*, suggests that the current classification does not fully reflect the phylogenetic relationships among these bacteria. The study implies that the existing genus designations may be incongruent with the underlying phylogeny. The proposed reclassification provides a more accurate representation of the evolutionary history of these thermophilic bacteria. The study highlights the importance of considering closely related genera together in phylogenetic analyses for accurate taxonomic classification. The unresolved placement of *A. vitaminiphilus* and *A. amylolyticus* suggests that further investigation is needed to clarify their taxonomic affiliations. The findings shift the understanding of the relationships among these genera, providing a more refined framework for future research.

Three Takeaways
The core novel contribution is the proposal of a new genus, *Quasigeobacillus*, based on a comprehensive phylogenomic analysis of the genera *Anoxybacillus*, *Geobacillus*, and *Parageobacillus*. The strongest evidence is the consistent clustering of a specific clade in the core gene tree and the ANI/AAI dendrograms, supported by the mean ANI and AAI values. The primary limitation is the unresolved taxonomic placement of *A. vitaminiphilus* and *A. amylolyticus*, which requires further investigation to fully resolve their phylogenetic relationships. A forward path includes expanding the dataset with additional genomes and exploring the functional characteristics of the unique genes identified in *Quasigeobacillus*.
