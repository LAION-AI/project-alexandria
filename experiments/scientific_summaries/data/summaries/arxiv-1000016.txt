Executive Summary
This paper investigates the spontaneous scalarization of Bardeen black holes, a phenomenon where these black holes develop scalar hair due to a tachyonic instability. The study introduces two scalar field couplings, quadratic (1 − αφ²) and exponential (e⁻αφ²), to model this instability. The authors find infinite branches of scalarized charged black holes (SCBHs) with magnetic charge *g*. The *n* = 0 branch of SCBHs emerges when the coupling parameter α exceeds a threshold, α ≥ α*n*=0(*g*), for both coupling forms. Importantly, the *n* = 0 branch is shown to be stable against radial perturbations. This stability is a key finding, suggesting that the *n* = 0 branch could have observational implications. The study uses numerical methods to analyze the stability of the *n* = 0 branch against radial perturbations, finding that the *n* = 0 branch is stable. The research builds on the concept of spontaneous scalarization, a strong gravity phase transition, and extends it to the context of Bardeen black holes, which are regular black hole solutions. The main limitation is that the stability analysis is limited to radial perturbations, and the study does not explore the dynamics of mergers or stellar core collapse. The findings are significant because they identify a stable branch of scalarized Bardeen black holes, which could be relevant for future observational studies.

Key Results
The study finds that the Bardeen black hole is unstable for α > α*n*=0(*g*), and stable for α < α*n*=0(*g*), where α*n*=0(*g*) is the threshold of instability. The threshold values are α*th*(*g*) = 17.338 (for *g* = 0.175), 12.712 (for *g* = 0.200), and 7.251 (for *g* = 0.250) (Figure 2). The *n* = 0 branch of SCBHs can be found for α ≥ α*n*=0(*g*) with both quadratic and exponential couplings. The bifurcation point α*n*=0(*g*) increases as *g* decreases. The *n* = 0 branch of SCBHs, obtained with both quadratic and exponential couplings, is stable against radial perturbations (Figures 5 and 6). For the quadratic coupling, the potentials around the *n* = 0 branch show small negative regions in the near-horizon, but the *n* = 0 black hole is stable against the *l* = 0 scalar mode (Figure 6(a)). For the exponential coupling, the *n* = 0 branch exhibits a large positive region outside the horizon, suggesting stability, and the *n* = 0 black hole is stable against the *l* = 0 scalar mode (Figure 6(b)). The study confirms that the stability (or instability) of *n* = 0 black holes is independent of the magnetic charge *g*. The metric function *N*(r) has a horizon at ln *r* = −0.0683, while *f*(r) for the Bardeen black hole takes a horizon at ln *r* = −0.0671 (Figure 4).

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that Bardeen black holes can undergo spontaneous scalarization, leading to the formation of stable SCBHs. The stability of the *n* = 0 branch implies that it represents a viable end state for the Bardeen black hole under certain conditions. The results support the idea that regular black holes, such as Bardeen black holes, can exhibit scalarization, expanding the understanding of black hole physics beyond the standard general relativity framework. The study implies that the tachyonic instability of Bardeen black holes, triggered by non-minimal scalar field coupling, leads to the formation of SCBHs. The stability of the *n* = 0 branch suggests that these scalarized black holes could be observed, opening up possibilities for testing alternative theories of gravity. The findings also suggest that the magnetic charge *g* influences the onset of the instability, with smaller magnetic charges making the instability harder to realize. This research contributes to the understanding of the behavior of black holes in the presence of scalar fields and nonlinear electrodynamics.

Contradictions Limitations
The study's primary limitation is its focus on radial perturbations, which restricts the scope of the stability analysis. The stability against other types of perturbations (e.g., non-radial or time-dependent) is not investigated. The study does not explore the nonlinear dynamics of scalarized black holes, such as mergers or stellar core collapse. The analysis is limited to the *n* = 0 branch of SCBHs, and the stability of other branches (n ≠ 0) is not examined, although the authors suggest that these other branches may be unstable. The paper does not provide details on the specific numerical methods or software used, which could impact reproducibility. The study does not consider the effects of rotation or other complexities that could influence the scalarization process. The absence of details on the numerical methods and the lack of exploration of other perturbation modes are key limitations. The study does not discuss potential conflicts with existing literature, but it builds upon and extends the existing research on black hole scalarization.
