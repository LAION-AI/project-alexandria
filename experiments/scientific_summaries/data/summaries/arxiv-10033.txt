Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel experimental approach to detect potential variations in the electron-to-proton mass ratio, µ = me/mp, by exploiting the sensitivity of molecular vibrational levels to changes in µ. The authors focus on Cs2 molecules, specifically examining the near-degeneracy between vibrational levels associated with different electronic potentials. The study demonstrates the existence of such level pairs in Cs2, which can amplify the sensitivity to changes in µ. The authors utilize two-color photoassociation spectroscopy to determine the positions of deeply bound a3Σ+u levels in Cs2. The key finding is the identification of a near-degeneracy between the a3Σ+u (va = 37) and X1Σ+g (vX = 138) levels, which is crucial for enhancing sensitivity. They estimate that a fractional change of ∆µ/µ ≲ 10−17 could be detected in approximately one day of integration. This work is significant because it provides a new method for probing fundamental physics by searching for variations in fundamental constants, potentially offering a more sensitive approach than existing methods. The primary limitation is the need for further experimental data to confirm the near-degeneracies and refine the sensitivity estimates.

Research Context
The search for variations in fundamental constants, such as the fine-structure constant α and the electron-to-proton mass ratio µ, is motivated by theoretical models, including those inspired by string theory and quintessence. Prior research has primarily focused on variations in α, with less attention given to µ, despite theoretical predictions suggesting that variations in µ could be more pronounced. Existing methods, such as examining molecular spectra from distant sources, face challenges related to systematic errors. This work addresses the need for a new laboratory experiment to search for variations in µ with high sensitivity, overcoming the limitations of previous approaches by utilizing molecular spectra in ultracold Cs2.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research question is: Can the sensitivity to variations in the electron-to-proton mass ratio, µ, be enhanced by exploiting the properties of molecular spectra? The authors hypothesize that the energy of molecular vibrational levels can be highly sensitive to changes in µ, and that the sensitivity can be further amplified by utilizing nearly-degenerate pairs of vibrational levels associated with different electronic potentials. They predict that the splitting between such levels in Cs2 can be measured with sufficient precision to detect a fractional change of ∆µ/µ ≲ 10−17. This approach differs from previous work by focusing on laboratory-based experiments using ultracold molecules, which offer the potential for improved control and reduced systematic errors compared to astrophysical observations. The successful identification of near-degenerate levels in Cs2 and the demonstration of their sensitivity to changes in µ would support the hypothesis. Conversely, if no suitable level pairs were found or if the sensitivity was significantly lower than predicted, the hypothesis would be challenged.

A uniform magnetic field is applied, and atoms are optically pumped into the 6s1/2 F = mF = 4 state. A CW laser (PA laser) excites the atoms into a bound state of the (2)0−g potential, which then decays to a manifold of a3Σ+u vibrational levels. A second CW (probe) laser is used to search for resonances between the a3Σ+u levels and the (2)0−g level excited by the PA laser. The probe laser frequency is scanned while monitoring the ion yield to detect resonant dips in the signal. The binding energy of the a3Σ+u level is determined from the difference between the frequencies of the resonant probe and PA lasers. The data analysis involves fitting the potentials to all known levels in both the a3Σ+u and X1Σ+g states, taking into account the SO2 interaction. Key Results
The study demonstrates the existence of near-degenerate vibrational levels in Cs2, specifically the a3Σ+u (va = 37) and X1Σ+g (vX = 138) levels. Figure 1 shows the energy sensitivity versus binding energy for the X1Σ+g and a3Σ+u states of Cs2. Figure 2 shows data indicating a degeneracy between the deeply bound a3Σ+u (va = 37) and X1Σ+g (vX = 138) levels in Cs2. The authors observed multiple sublevels for several a3Σ+u vibrational levels with |Eb(v)|∼400−1500 GHz. The root mean square deviation was approximately 90 MHz for HF/rotational/SO2-induced sublevel splittings, and approximately 200 MHz for absolute binding energies. The authors estimate that a fractional change of ∆µ/µ ≲ 10−17 could be achieved in approximately one day of integration. The authors predict a magnetic dipole transition between the observed level a|(1, 7)6, 4, 10⟩ and X|(0, 6)6, 4, 10⟩, at Ω ≈ 6.3 GHz, with transition amplitude M ≈ 0.1 µB. They also predict an E1 transition between the observed level a|(1, 7)8, 2, 10⟩ and X|(0, 7)7, 3, 10⟩ at Ω ≈ 9.8 GHz, with M ≈ 1.5 × 10−4 ea0 ≈ 0.04 µB. The study found that the energy sensitivity ∂µE(X)vX increases monotonically with |Eb|, throughout the region of a state levels.

Interpretation Implications
The findings suggest that molecular spectroscopy, particularly in systems like Cs2, can be a sensitive probe for variations in the electron-to-proton mass ratio. The near-degeneracy of vibrational levels enhances the sensitivity, providing a mechanism to amplify the effects of changes in µ. The results support the theoretical expectation that variations in µ could be detectable and potentially more significant than variations in the fine-structure constant. The study implies that laboratory-based experiments can offer a viable alternative to astrophysical observations for probing fundamental constants. The proposed approach could lead to a deeper understanding of fundamental physics and potentially constrain models of dark energy and extra dimensions.
