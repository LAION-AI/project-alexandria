Executive Summary
This paper introduces a discrete model for studying scattering in one-dimensional, PT-symmetric quantum mechanics using a Runge-Kutta-discretized version of the Schrödinger equation. The author derives formulas for reflection and transmission coefficients, highlighting that the primary innovation stems from the complexification of potential-characterizing parameters. The study employs a matching method and a matrix-inversion method to solve the scattering problem, focusing on the coefficients α and β, which encapsulate the dynamical input information. The key finding is the formal parallelism between scattering descriptions for real (Hermitian) and complex, PT-symmetric potentials, with differences residing in the form of the dynamical input. The model demonstrates that the asymmetry between "in" and "out" states, a characteristic of PT-symmetric quantum mechanics, is merely formal and can be addressed using standard linear algebra techniques. The study's significance lies in its demonstration that the scattering problem in PT-symmetric quantum mechanics remains tractable through standard methods, despite its non-Hermitian nature, and its potential for further investigation into non-Hermitian versions of the inverse scattering problem. The primary limitation is the focus on a simplified, discrete model, which may not fully capture the complexities of continuous systems.

The matching method, applied with M=1, yields the reflection coefficient B = Z0 / (2i sin ϕ − Z0) and the transmission coefficient C = 2i sin ϕ / (2i sin ϕ − Z0).
