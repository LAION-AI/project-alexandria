Executive Summary
This paper introduces an efficient decoder architecture for semantic segmentation in automated driving applications, focusing on real-time performance on embedded platforms. The study addresses the computational expense of decoders in semantic segmentation, particularly in contrast to the more task-agnostic nature of efficient encoders. The authors propose a novel non-bottleneck layer and a family of decoders designed to work with a VGG10 encoder, aiming to fit within a constrained run-time budget. The methodology involves designing and evaluating various decoder configurations using a custom dataset of urban scenes. The primary finding is that the optimal decoder configuration, incorporating the proposed non-bottleneck layer, achieves a 10% improvement in mean Intersection over Union (IoU) score compared to a baseline configuration. This improvement is more pronounced for challenging classes like lanes (+12% IoU) and curb (+18% IoU). The novelty lies in the design of an efficient decoder that can improve segmentation accuracy while maintaining real-time performance. The work is significant because it highlights the importance of decoder design in achieving efficient and accurate semantic segmentation for resource-constrained applications like automated driving. A key limitation is the use of a task-independent encoder, which may not fully exploit the potential of the overall architecture.

Research Context
Semantic segmentation, crucial for applications like automated driving, faces computational challenges, especially on embedded systems. Existing research has focused on efficient encoder design, but decoders are also computationally expensive. Prior methods include architectures like FCN, SegNet, and UNet, which often mirror the encoder's complexity in the decoder or use skip connections. These methods do not necessarily prioritize efficiency for real-time applications. This work addresses the gap by focusing on efficient decoder design, assuming a task-independent encoder, and aiming for real-time performance on embedded platforms. This contrasts with existing approaches that either focus on encoder efficiency or use computationally expensive decoders.

Research Question Hypothesis
The central research question is how to design an efficient decoder for semantic segmentation that maintains accuracy while meeting real-time constraints in automated driving. The primary hypothesis is that a novel non-bottleneck layer, integrated into a family of decoder architectures, will improve segmentation performance compared to a baseline configuration, while remaining computationally efficient. The study predicts that the proposed decoder will achieve higher IoU scores, particularly for challenging classes like lanes and curb, without significantly increasing run-time. Alternative hypotheses could include that the proposed non-bottleneck layer does not improve performance, or that the performance gains are offset by increased computational cost. The research differs from prior work by prioritizing decoder efficiency and assuming a task-independent encoder. The study aims to demonstrate that a well-designed decoder can significantly impact segmentation accuracy, even with a fixed encoder, and that the proposed non-bottleneck layer can effectively handle the challenges of semantic segmentation in urban scenes.

Methodological Details
The study employs an empirical approach, evaluating different decoder architectures on a custom dataset. The dataset, owned by the authors' organization, comprises 1280x384 pixel images of urban scenes, including diverse lighting conditions and shadows. The dataset is divided into training (3016 images), validation (981 images), and test (1002 images) sets. The semantic segmentation task focuses on four classes: lanes, curb, road, and void. The encoder used is a VGG10-style classifier. The proposed decoder architectures are built using a novel non-bottleneck layer. The models are implemented using the Keras framework. The Adam optimizer is used, with a weight decay of 0.9 and a batch size of 4 (or 8, depending on the decoder variant). The initial learning rate is 0.0005, with a standard polynomial decay strategy over 350,000 iterations. Dropout is not used. Experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA Titan X 12G GPU with 24 GB RAM. No data augmentation is performed. The performance of different decoder variants is evaluated using the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric. The study compares the performance of the proposed decoder variants against a standard FCN decoder. The hardware used is designed with automotive power constraints in mind. The training process involves pre-training the encoder on ImageNet and then transferring the weights for the semantic segmentation task. The study does not mention the random seed used.

Procedures Architectures
The study focuses on designing an efficient decoder for semantic segmentation, assuming a task-independent encoder. The overall architecture follows an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder, a VGG10-style classifier, extracts features from the input images. The decoder reconstructs the original input space and generates class predictions for each pixel. The decoder uses deconvolution layers for reconstruction, instead of max-unpooling. The core innovation is the proposed non-bottleneck layer, which is designed to handle high gradient flow and learn semantic information from the encoded feature space. The non-bottleneck layer has two variants (Type-1 and Type-2). Type-1 uses 1D and 3D kernels, while Type-2 incorporates dilated convolutions. The architecture uses multiple skip connections to distribute gradients during backpropagation. The encoder-decoder architecture is illustrated in Figure 3. The encoder downsamples feature maps from 256 to 4. The decoder architecture consists of deconvolution layers and non-bottleneck layers. The optimal decoder configuration uses two non-bottleneck layers of Type-1, followed by two non-bottleneck layers of Type-2, and then one more non-bottleneck layer of Type-2. Intermediate feature maps from the second and third-to-last convolution layers of the encoder are also used. The encoder consumes most of the run-time. The design avoids the use of group convolution, as it was found to have adverse effects for lightweight networks. The architecture is designed for real-time performance on embedded platforms.

Three Takeaways
