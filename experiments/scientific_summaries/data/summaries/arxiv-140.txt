Executive Summary
This paper revisits Moore's work on real-valued recursive functions, which aimed to model analog computation, and demonstrates that a key claim relating real primitive recursive functions to differentially algebraic functions is incorrect. Moore's definition of these function classes, inspired by Kleene's work on standard recursive functions, contained ambiguities, particularly regarding partial functions. The author clarifies these definitions, especially the differential recursion operator, and proves that real primitive recursive functions, as defined with a more restrictive domain, are not necessarily differentially algebraic. This refutes Moore's claim that real primitive recursive functions are differentially algebraic and, by extension, related to Shannon's model of analog computation. The paper also discusses the implications of this finding, including the need for a new approach to relate real primitive recursive functions to differentially algebraic functions and the challenges in defining real recursive functions with the minimization operator. The main contribution is the formalization of Moore's theory and the demonstration that the purported connection between real primitive recursive functions and differentially algebraic functions fails. The primary limitation is the loss of the direct link to differentially algebraic functions, which necessitates further research to find a suitable subclass. The results suggest that the original claims about the relationship between Moore's function classes and analog computation need to be reevaluated, and the paper proposes open problems for future research.
