I propose a curved spacetime version of the Dirac equation.
The equation
has been developed mainly to try and account in a natural way for the observed anomalous
gyromagnetic ratio of Fermions. The derived equation suggests that particles including the
Electron which is thought to be a point particle do have a ﬁnite spatial size which is the
reason for the observed anomalous gyromagnetic ratio. A serendipitous result of the theory,
is that it exhibits an asymmetry in its positive and negative energy solutions, the suggestion
of which is clear that a solution to the problem as to why we do not observe negative energy
and mass particles in the part of our measurable and accessible Universe is possible.
Keywords:
Curved Space, Dirac Equation, Gyromagnetic Ratio, Fundamental Parti-
cle.

I.
INTRODUCTION
The Dirac equation is a relativistic quantum mechanical wave
equation invented by Paul Dirac in 1928 (Dirac 1928a, 1928b)
originally designed to overcome the criticism of the Klein-
Gordon Equation. The Klein-Gordon equation gave negative
probabilities and this is considered to be physically meaning-
less. Despite this fact, this equation accounts well for Bosons,
that is spin zero particles. This criticism leveled against the
Klein-Gordon equation, motivated Dirac to successfully seek
an equation devoid of negative probabilities.
The Dirac equation is consistent with Quantum Mechanics
(QM) and fully consistent with the Special Theory of Rela-
tivity (STR). The equation accounts in a natural way for the
nature of particle spin as a relativistic phenomenon. The equa-
tion successfully predicted the existence of anti-particles. In
its bare form, the equation provides an impressive and accu-
rate description of the Electron hence it being referred in most
of the literature as the “Dirac Equation for the Electron”. It
also accounts well for quarks and other spin half particles al-

though in some of the cases, there is need for slight modiﬁca-
tions - for example, you need the Procca equation to describe
the neutron.
The ﬁrst taste of glory of the Dirac equation was it being able
to account for the gyromagnetic ratio of the electron, that is
g = 2, which can not be accounted for using non-relativistic
QM. For several years after it’s discovery, most physicists be-
lieved that it also described the Proton and the Neutron, which
are both spin-1/2 particles. In simple terms, it was thought or
presumed that the Dirac equation was a universal equation for
spin-1/2 particles.
However, beginning with the experiments of Stern and Frisch
in 1933, the magnetic moments of these particles were found
to disagree signiﬁcantly with the predictions of the Dirac
equation. The Proton was found to have a gyromagnetic ratio
gp = 5.58 which is 2.79 times larger than that predicted by
the Dirac equation. The Neutron, which is electrically neu-
tral spin-1/2 particle was found to have a gyromagnetic ratio
gn = −3.83.
These “anomalous magnetic moments” of the Neutron and
Proton which are clearly not conformatory to the Dirac theory
have been taken to be experimental indication that these par-
tices are not fundamental particles. In the case of the Neutron,
yes it is clearly not a fundamental particle since it does decay

into a Proton, Electron and Neutrino, that is, n −→p + e−+ ν.
If the Dirac equation is a universal equation for fundamen-
tal fermion particles, then any fundamental fermion particle
must conform to this equation. Simple, any spin-1/2 particle
that can not be described by it, must therefore not be a funda-
mental particle of nature. By deﬁnition a fundamental particle
is a particle known to have no sub-structure, that is, it can not
be broken down into smaller particles thus will not decay into
anything else.
From the Standard Model, we know that the Proton and Neu-
tron are composed of quarks thus are not fundamental parti-
cles. The question is, is this the reason why these particle’s
gyromagnetic ratio is diﬀerent from that predicted by the bare
Dirac equation? Prevailing wisdom suggests that anomalous
gyromagnetic ratio arise because the particles under question
are not fundamental particles. From the theory laid down here,
the answer to this is a clear no. The reason for the deviation
of the gyromagnetic ratio from that expected from the Dirac
theory is (according to this theory) because particles do have
a ﬁnite size and that spacetime is curved. In this theory the
anomalous gyromagnetic ratio arises from the interaction of
spin with the Lorentz force in a curved spacetime for a parti-
cle of ﬁnite spatial size and mass. The derived relation for the
anomalous magnetic moment and the particle size is similar to
that deduced by Brodsky & Drell (1980) and experimentally
veriﬁed by Dehmelt (1989). Brodsky & Drell (1980) proposed
that fermions do have a sub-structure and this gives rise to the
anomalous gyromagnetic ratio which varies as the spatial size
and inverse to the mass.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
Amongst others, the most important consequence of the the-
ory just laid down of the curved space-time Dirac equation it
exhibits and asymmetry in the energy solution and the sugges-
tion from it, is that the problem of negative energy and mass
that lead Dirac to propose his Hole Theory or Dirac Sea, in an
eﬀort to explain why is it that we do not observe negative en-
ergy and mass in the Universe is no longer, may be solved but
this will be done in a third paper. From the said, then, if the
present theory is a true reﬂection and/or description of natural
reality or anything to go by, then it explains the long standing
problem since Dirac put forward his Hole Theory or the Dirac
Sea Hypothesis in 1930 in an eﬀort to explain the anomalous
negative-energy spectrum as predicted by his theory, of why
the Universe as we know it, appears to be ﬁlled with positive
energy and mass. Dirac’s theory predicted the existence of
anti-particles which have been “observed” but not in the sense
in which they are predicted by the theory in that they must
have a negative mass/energy.
Further, the Dirac theory put it bold that these anti-particles
should have a negative mass and energy. To this present day, it
has not been conﬁrmed whether anti-particles have a negative
mass and energy but the suspicion amongst the majority of
physicists is that anti-particles do have a positive mass and
energy – hence the quotations “observed”. As suggested, this
the asymmetry in energy solution of equation 12 can be used
to solve this problem and this will be done in the Paper II.
There in Paper II, it will be shown that anti-particles as we
know them have a positive mass.
Another of the predictions of the theory is the existence of four
particle solutions each with the same electric charge and gy-
romagnetic ratio. The four solutions are such that the ﬁrst pair
have an energy E+ the spins of which are opposite and the sec-
ond having energy E−and again the the spins of which are op-
posite. This suggests that a particle will have a partner which
is heavier or lighter than itself. On this stratum, this brings
one to think of the Electron and the Muon as a particle heavy-
partner pair since they have the same electronic charge and
gyromagnetic ratios. Begining with Dirac in 1962, the Muon
has long been thought of as an excited state of the Electron
because these two particles appear to have the same physical
properties because their interactions in nature are very similar
and it is for this reason that often the Muon is thought of as an
extremely heavy electron.
One can check that equation 23 has all the symmetry proper-
ties that are possessed by the ﬂat spacetime Dirac equation.
One important such symmetry that I wish to brieﬂy consider
is the charge conjugation symmetry. Because of this, it means
that particles with the opposite sign of the electric charge are
predicted by this equation and these by deﬁnition in the Dirac
sense are the anti-particles with the exception that they have a
positive mass and energy! This suggests that the unstable e+
is the charge conjugated partner of the stable e−. This theory
does not explain why the other of the charge conjugated part-
ner is unstable. Furthermore, another unfortunate thing about
the present theory is that it does not explain why anti-particles
(in the present sense of partner, heavy-partner pair) are not
observed naturally in that accessable part of the Universe that
we live. That is to say, if both the Electron and Muon are
fundamental particles, why then is the Muon semi-stable? To
answer this, let us endore the vacuum with some energy Evac.
This means that all the energy states up to Evac are ﬁlled. Now
take a particle and its heavier-partner. We can have three sit-
uations: (1) where both (E+, E−) < Evac, in which case both
the particle and its heavier-partner will exist in a stable state.
(2) where E+ > Evac and E−< Evac in this case the heavier-
partner will be semi stable and will decay to the lower energy
state of the lighter partner (3) where (E+, E−) > Evac, in this
case both the particle and its heavier-partner will be unstable
and there is no forseeable way how these can exist naturally.
In this case, the, reason therefore the Muon is unstable is be-
cause of the vacuum energy being less than its total energy.
Exploring, from a rather heuristic viewpoint, the above ideas
further, we know that according to the currently accepted
Standard Big-Bang theory – a theory of the origin, evolution
and fate of the Universe, the vacuum energy should have in
the past been much higher than it is today. This in actual
fact means, back in time Muons should have been much more
stable than they are today thus may have existed naturally
much the same way has Electrons exist today. If this is the
case, fossil evidence of such a scenario will most probably be
archived in the form of the existence of a bath of the Muon
and Electron-neutrino since µ−−→e−+ ¯νe + νµ. These are
merely suggestions.
In closing, I could like to say it is interesting to know what
kind of results will emerge when equation 23 is used for do-
ing QED and the Standard Model instead of the normal Dirac
equation of ﬂat space time. Will equation 23 be able to ex-
plain certain observations which have required us to modify
the original Dirac equation inorder to explain them? With this
equation, it is clear that one will need not the Feynman dia-
grams to explain anomalies that are not naturally explained by
the bare Dirac theory.
