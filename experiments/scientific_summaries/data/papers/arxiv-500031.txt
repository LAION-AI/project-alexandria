B-Mode auto-bispectrum due to matter bounce
Rahul Kothari∗and Debottam Nandi†
Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai 600036, India
In this article, we investigate CMB B-mode auto-bispectrum due to matter bounce Universe. It is known
that this mode can only be generated by tensor perturbations, i.e., the gravity waves. For simplicity, we study
the B-modes in a minimally coupled Einstein frame. We obtain the analytical expression for the B-mode auto-
bispectrum and numerically evaluate it. We ﬁnd that, similar to inﬂation, the signal-to-noise ratio is small and
difﬁcult to detect in the future experiments. To ease the numerical evaluation of the bispectrum, we develop
and use various techniques. We believe that these techniques can be used in various contexts other than just
calculating the bispectrum.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMB) is
considered to be a relic of events happened in the early Uni-
verse. Primordial ﬂuctuations generated in the early Uni-
verse leave imprints on the CMB. Thus CMB helps us deci-
pher the nature of these primordial perturbations.
These perturbations can be related to the spherical har-
monic coefﬁcients of the CMB ﬁeld through transfer func-
tions. Thus we can evolve the primordial perturbations, start-
ing from the beginning to recombination and then to the
present epoch. With the help of correlation function of these
harmonic coefﬁcients, we can infer about the state of the
early universe. CMB ﬁeld, in addition to being statistically
isotropic, is also assumed to be gaussian to a large extent.
Thus, all the statistical information is contained in the two
point correlations of its harmonic coefﬁcients. This is be-
cause any higher even order correlation can be expressed
in terms of two point correlations using Wick’s Theorem.
Moreover, all odd correlations turn out to be zero. How-
ever, in the presence of non-gaussianities in the primordial
ﬂuctuations, odd correlations are nonzero as well. The study
of these primordial non-gaussianities provides an important
information about the physics of the early universe. Thus it
may help us put tighter constraints on early Universe models.
Amongst such models, within the framework of standard
model of cosmology, inﬂationary paradigm is the most re-
markable one [1–5]. Its success is not only based on provid-
ing a nearly scale invariant spectra as required by observa-
tions, but also in solving the horizon and ﬂatness problems.
During inﬂation, vacuum ﬂuctuations are generated and get
ampliﬁed which eventually leads to the generation of large
scale structures. However, even with tighter constraints, we
are unable to rule out sufﬁcient number of models within the
the inﬂationary paradigm [6–10]. Therefore, there is a grow-
ing interest in ﬁnding new alternatives to inﬂation. One such
popular paradigms is bounce [11–16].
Bouncing cosmologies have been present in the scientiﬁc
literature since late 70’s [11]. Bouncing models represent
the situations where the universe initially undergoes a period
of contraction until the scale factor reaches a minima, after

One speciﬁc kind of bouncing scenarios, known as matter

as η2, i.e., in the same way as it varies in the matter domi-

difﬁcult to distinguish between these two paradigms due to
the fact that power spectrum at the end of both paradigms
is the same. However, the perturbations act differently in
both paradigms. In standard slow-roll inﬂation, perturbations
freeze outside the horizon but in matter bounce perturbations
grow even outside the horizon. On account of this very fact,
characteristic behaviours can be seen in the higher order cor-
relation functions. In Ref. [20], authors studied the B-mode
auto-bispectrum in a generalized slow-roll inﬂation model
and showed that only higher order Galileons can boost the
tensor bispectra. In this work, we focus on matter bounce
[21]. We study the B-mode auto-bispectrum generated due
to matter bounce and compare it with the standard slow-roll
inﬂation.
The paper is structured in the following manner. In the
next section, we obtain the relation of three point correla-
tion function of spherical harmonic coefﬁcients in terms of
the three point function of primordial perturbations. Ana-
lytic form of bispectrum due to matter bounce is obtained
in section III. This, in section IV, is followed by a detailed
discussion of the numerical computation techniques for the
evaluation of the bispectrum. The computation is done with
the help of the transfer functions obtained from the CAMB1
software. In our analysis, we have used Planck 2018 cos-
mological parameters [22]. In section V, we summarize the
results. Finally, in section VI, we conclude this paper by
comparing our results with the same in standard slow-roll
inﬂation scenario.

B.
Wigner Symbols’ Computation
We now provide details about the Wigner 3j calculation.
Since 9j symbols can be expressed in terms of 3j symbols, the
discussion of 3j would sufﬁce. We employed two methods
for computation.
The ﬁrst method is the Rasch Algorithm [30].
This is
based on the Regge symmetries of the Wigner 3j symbols.
The Rasch method employs 36 out of 72 symmetries3. Thus
by computing and storing one symbol, 36 other symbols are
determined as well. This considerably reduces the computa-
tion time. For the evaluation of the symbols, we use fgsl4
which is a fortran version of gsl5. But it turned out that fgsl
gives segmentation fault at larger values of l. So we use
wigxjpf for the bispectrum evaluation at larger values of l
while calculating SNR.
Wigxjpf software [31] calculates these symbols using
prime number factorization.

VI.
CONCLUSION
In this paper, we have analytically calculated the B-mode
auto-bispectrum due to matter bounce. We also have com-
puted the bispectrum numerically and compared it with that
generated due to standard inﬂationary paradigm. The numer-
ical computation of bispectrum involves integration over 4
(or 5) integrals. Since the bispectrum exhibits symmetries
in (l1,l2,l3), the computation time can be reduced to 1/6.
The bispectrum expression (12) contains summation over
Li’s which, in turn, are dependent upon li’s, as can be seen in
Eq. (13). This gives us advantage of evaluating k integrals
only for valid (l,L) pairs, i.e., Eq. (14). This was followed by
the x integration. In order to avoid the repetitive calculation
of same k integrals, we saved the integral (14) information as
a function of x on an array. To serve this purpose, we devel-
oped a method for storing and retrieving data, using a binary
search method for integer n tuples. Two theorems provided
by us ensure the certainty of the binary search method. For
efﬁcient evaluation, we ﬁrst calculated the Wigner symbols
by using the open source softwares, e.g., fgsl and wigxjpf.
Then, we stored and retrieve the required symbols using the
Rasch Algorithm. We think that the software wigxjpf used
for the evaluation of Wigner symbols can be coupled with
Rasch Algorithm to improve the computation speed.
